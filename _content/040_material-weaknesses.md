### GAAP, GAAS, and the division of responsibility
Below is a division of responsibility in relation to an audit.

The MUD's management (board with consultant support) is responsible for fair presentation of the financial statements in accordance with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) and for relevant internal control. 

The MUD's accounting and reporting policies (as administered by the board, bookkeeper, or an appropriate consultant) must conform to [GAAP for state and local governments](https://www.gasb.org/page/ShowPdf?path=gasbs76_final_cropped.pdf) as defined by the [Governmental Accounting Standards Board (GASB)](https://www.gasb.org/). 

The auditor is responsible for expressing an opinion based on an audit conducted under [Generally Accepted Auditing Standards (GAAS)](https://auditingauthority.com/gaas-generally-accepted-auditing-standards/). The auditor assesses risks of material misstatement and designs audit procedures accordingly. 

### The 2025 audit findings
The three-page [internal-control communication](/assets/docs/Internal-Control.pdf) issued with the [2025 MUD2 audit](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/static/aca54eeea16d936f13749fa12691dea7/Audit_FYE_2025_Final_Audit_Report_GLMUD_2_1_a83e444f68.pdf) for [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) deserves close attention. Issued by auditor [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.us/), the document is dated January 9, 2026, and titled **Communication Regarding Internal Control-Related Matters Identified in an Audit**.

While the audit opinion says that the financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the MUD's financial position, in accordance with GAAP, the [communication](/assets/docs/Internal-Control.pdf) lists important limitations that must be preserved whenever the audit's findings are discussed publicly.
- The auditor's consideration of internal control was performed while planning and conducting the financial-statement audit, but that it was not an opinion, or for the purpose of expressing an opinion, on the effectiveness of internal control. 
- The work was not designed to identify every possible material weakness or significant deficiency.
- Required supplementary information received only limited procedures and the auditor does not express an opinion or provide assurance on that information.
- The auditor did not subject the MUD's response to its [communication](/assets/docs/Internal-Control.pdf) to the auditing procedures applied in the audit and, accordingly, expresses no opinion on it.

### What is a material weakness?
The auditor's document defines a material weakness as a deficiency, or combination of deficiencies, in internal control such that there is a reasonable possibility that material misstatements of the audited entity's financial statements will not be prevented or detected and corrected on a timely basis. 

A material weakness means the control system has a sufficiently serious vulnerability that material financial-statement errors may not be prevented or detected and corrected promptly. Whether or not the corresponding bookkeeping could be "intentional," "fraud," "misconduct," "illegal," or "criminal" is something left to the appropriate agencies ([Harris County District Attorney's Office](https://dao.harriscountytx.gov/), [FBI Houston](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/houston), [Texas Attorney General](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/), [Texas Commission on Environmental Quality](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/), [Texas State Board of Public Accountancy](https://www.tsbpa.texas.gov/), [State Auditor's Office](https://sao.texas.gov/), and others) to investigate.

### The three material weaknesses the auditor identified

#### 1. Financial-statement preparation and expertise
The auditor says that one primary control in the internal-control system concerns preparation of the financial statements. According to the [communication](/assets/docs/Internal-Control.pdf), management is responsible either for preparing the statements or for having enough knowledge to determine that they have been properly prepared and are free of potential misstatement.

The auditor then states that the absence of this expertise within management, or within a consultant hired to perform the service, is considered a material weakness. This is significant because it concerns the **foundation of the reporting process itself**.

#### 2. Necessary accounting adjustments
The second weakness concerns adjustments. The auditor says that during the audit it is not unusual for the auditor to prepare journal entries to correct and present the financial statements on the government-wide basis. The auditor also says it identified adjustments to accounts including accounts payable and property-tax revenues that were necessary for the fund financial statements to conform with GAAP.

The inability of management, or a consultant hired to perform the service, to detect those necessary adjustments was classified as a material weakness. This gives us, the Grand Lakes residents, a concrete line of inquiry: **What were the adjustments? What are the corresponding amounts? What is the impact?**

The 2025 audit [communication](/assets/docs/Internal-Control.pdf) provides the categories of adjustments elsewhere in the report and states that there were **no uncorrected misstatements**. The residents note this fact alongside the material weaknesses, but do not consider the weakness cleared because nobody knows why revenues, capital assets, liabilities, etc., were misreported and what the impact would have been had the auditor not catch the misstatements.

- Accounts payable.
- Property-tax revenues.
- Accrued revenues.
- Capital assets and depreciation expense.
- Long-term liabilities.
- Conveyance of capital assets.

#### 3. Capital assets and depreciation
The third weakness concerns the capital-asset and depreciation register. The auditor states that management, or a consultant, does not prepare the register or post adjustments related to the presentation of capital assets in the government-wide financial statements. The auditor says management is not preparing or reviewing the information and does not have the expertise to prevent, detect, and correct related significant potential misstatements. The auditor classifies this as a material weakness.

To the Grand Lakes community with 2,740 households and 40 businesses, the proper public question is not "Who is guilty?" It is: **Who prepares the register, who reviews it, who approves changes, and what evidence supports the reported balances?**

### The MUD's response
The MUD's response to the auditor's [communication](/assets/docs/Internal-Control.pdf) is important and should be analyzed carefully. The MUD states that:

- The auditor's Management Letter and the material weaknesses were prepared in response to SAS 115 and were not prompted by other circumstances identified during the audit and disclosed to the Board.

- Since the district's inception, the Board has engaged the auditor to perform the audit and prepare the financial statements and capital-asset/depreciation schedules on which the audit is based. This is the usual and customary practice for smaller special districts in Texas, as it is more cost-effective than employing people to perform those functions. 

- The Board has not implemented controls to prevent, detect, and correct a misstatement in the preparation of the district’s financial statements or capital asset and depreciation schedules. Under the SAS 115 requirements, it engages the auditor to perform these services.

- The district's bookkeeper presents periodic unaudited financial information (income, disbursements, adjustments, and the status of investments) to the Board for review.

### What an audit opinion says and does not say
A financial statement can receive an unqualified opinion while the audit communication identifies material weaknesses in internal control. The 2025 MUD2 audit does **not**, by itself, establish intentional fraud, misstatement, or misconduct. It does establish something narrower and more concrete: **The auditor identified three material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting and six categories that underwent audit adjustments, but no uncorrected misstatements remained.** 

For a community trying to understand its districts and how the financial statements are produced and reviewed, that is just a starting point. Moving away from general impressions, and given the circumstances, residents are entitled to ask a number of documentable questions:

- Who prepared the MUD's financial statements before they reached the auditor?
- Who prepares the MUD's capital-asset register?
- Who reviews such statements before being shared with the auditor?
- Are such statements reviewed by a senior accountant or a CPA before being shared with the auditor?
- What were the specific items the auditor requested correcting;
    how many items were they;
    what were the amounts per entry;
    under which category (property-tax revenues, accrued revenues, expenses, capital assets, depreciation,
    liabilities) did each adjustment fall?
- What impact would the misreported items have had, individually and combined?
- Did the bookkeeper have similar or other misstatements in previous years?
- Did the auditor report the same or other weaknesses in previous years?
- Why did the auditor not request corrections / adjustments in previous years when it is obvious that
   the auditor’s concerns did not suddenly show up?
- Why did the auditor not report material weaknesses in previous years?
- Is there any evidence of the bookkeeper purposely misreporting the entries the auditor complained about?
- What controls exist now to prevent recurrence?

Those questions must be answered reliably from the workpapers, management letters, board packets, invoices, contracts, and general ledgers than from the audit opinion alone.
