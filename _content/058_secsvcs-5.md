Across Parts [I](./054_secsvcs-1) through [IV](./057_secsvcs-4) of this series, we documented how Grand Lakes water districts spent **$3.16 million** between FY2023 and FY2025 on "additional" Sheriff and Constable contracts, how records surrounding those renewals were shielded behind closed doors, and how general counsel [Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) ([Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/)) repeatedly pressured boards to renew those expenses for *"just one more year."*

To understand **why** a network of private attorneys, operators, bookkeepers, auditors, financial advisors, engineers, tax collectors, and other consultants works so relentlessly to drain cash reserves from a public utility district, you have to look at the structural finish line every Municipal Utility District (MUD) was created to reach: **paying off its debt, transferring its infrastructure, and dissolving.**

When you examine the financial conditions of [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) alongside its [**Strategic Partnership Agreement (SPA)**](/assets/docs/SPA_mud2.pdf) with the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/), the motive behind the depletion becomes unmistakable.

### The Five Conditions for Dissolution Are Effectively Met
A Texas MUD is not meant to be a permanent, self-perpetuating bureaucracy. It is a temporary financing vehicle designed to fund water, sewer, and drainage infrastructure until the development matures and the district can be absorbed into a municipality. 

Under the framework governing Grand Lakes and its [Strategic Partnership Agreement](/assets/docs/SPA_mud2.pdf) with the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/), the core prerequisites for full-purpose annexation and dissolution are already satisfied—or at the finish line:

* **Strategic Partnership Agreement in Place:** A formal SPA with the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) already governs the District’s annexation timeline and revenue sharing.
* **Bond Debt Paid Off:** The District’s long-term bond debt has been fully paid. There is **zero bond debt** left for the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) to assume upon annexation.
* **No Delinquent Financial Obligations:** The District is current on its obligations, with no unpaid debts that would legally obstruct dissolution.
* **Approved Utility & Drainage Infrastructure:** The District’s water, wastewater, and drainage systems—which transfer into the City’s public infrastructure system upon dissolution—have already been constructed and approved to municipal standards.
* **Developer Reimbursements Cleared (FY2026):** Audit reports show remaining capital expenditure obligations—reflecting final reimbursements owed to the developer for installing District infrastructure—declining steadily from **$1,032,506** (FY2023) to **$463,034** (FY2024), and down to just **$219,427** in FY2025. That minimal remaining balance was scheduled to be **fully paid off by August 31, 2026**.

In short: **the District’s debt-retirement mission is complete.**

### The March 2035 Conversion Date—And the Option to Dissolve Even Sooner
Under the terms of the [Strategic Partnership Agreement](/assets/docs/SPA_mud2.pdf) with the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/):
* The District is scheduled for **full-purpose annexation conversion in March 2035**, at which point the MUD dissolves and its utility operations merge into the City.
* **Crucially, the District does not have to wait until 2035:** Upon request by the MUD’s own Board of Directors, the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) may terminate the SPA and initiate full-purpose annexation proceedings **on any date prior to March 2035**.
* Conversely, the SPA also allows the March 2035 conversion date to be **altered by mutual agreement** of the District and the City.

### Why "Draining the Cash" Is Existential for Consultants
For Grand Lakes taxpayers, full-purpose annexation and dissolution end decades of unaccountable consultant fees, opaque contracting, and redundant multi-district overhead.

For the entrenched network of private vendors—attorneys, operators, engineers, bookkeepers, and financial advisors—**dissolution is an extinction event.** Once the MUD dissolves, their recurring monthly retainers, legal billings, and bond-issuance windfalls stop forever.

Look at how every piece of "The Mechanics of Depletion" serves to prevent dissolution or manufacture an excuse to extend the SPA past March 2035:

| District Financial State | Impact on Dissolution & SPA | Who Benefits? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Debt-Free & Cash-Healthy** *(The natural state once bonds and developer reimbursements are paid off)* | Clean, immediate transition to [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) annexation—either ahead of schedule or on time in **March 2035**. | **Grand Lakes Taxpayers** (Lower overhead, end to consultant retainers, municipal accountability). |
| **Cash-Drained & Facing Insolvency** *(Created by $1.1M+/yr security contracts and unbudgeted outlays)* | Operating reserves are exhausted within three years, forcing new bond debt or emergency rate hikes—creating a pretext to **amend the SPA and delay annexation past 2035**. | **Private Consultants & Vendors** (Years of additional retainers, legal fees, and new bond-issuance commissions). |

### A Clear Line for the Board and Residents
Extending or altering the **March 2035** full-purpose annexation conversion date would be a catastrophic mistake for Grand Lakes residents. It would serve zero public purpose—its only function would be to keep a lifeline open for consultants who are actively driving a debt-free district toward financial distress.

Instead of rubber-stamping "one more year" of $1.1M+ security contracts or approving measures that drain cash reserves, Grand Lakes directors should be doing the exact opposite:
* **Halt all non-essential cash depletion immediately** so the District maintains healthy operating reserves.
* **Reject any proposal to extend the SPA past March 2035.**
* **Evaluate formally requesting early SPA termination and annexation proceedings** now that bond debt and developer reimbursements have reached the finish line.

*Grand Lakes residents built and paid for this community’s infrastructure. It is time to protect the equity taxpayers funded—and bring the era of consultant-driven depletion to an end.*
