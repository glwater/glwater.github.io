Under Texas Water Code procurement rules, public construction contracts awarded via competitive sealed bids are intended to protect taxpayers by selecting the lowest responsible bidder. Yet in December 2025, [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) recommended—and [Grand Lakes MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/) later approved—a contractor whose total bid was **$140,644 higher** than the lowest bidder, with zero written justification in the [recommendation letter](/assets/docs/20251210_PDltr.pdf).

### The $140,644 Price Gap
On December 2, 2025, [Grand Lakes MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/) opened eight sealed bids for the **Re-Coating of Ground Storage Tank (GST) No. 1 at Water Plant No. 1** ([Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) Project No. 14041429-006). In a [December 10, 2025 letter](/assets/docs/20251210_PDltr.pdf), [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) Project Manager Nicholas Pratka recommended awarding the contract to the **second bidder, [CROM CFG Industrial LLC](https://www.cromcorp.com/)**, for **$594,350.00** (including Alternate No. 3 for an umbrella roof). However, Pape-Dawson’s own Bid Tabulation tells a starkly different story:

| Bidder Name | Base Bid | Supplemental Items | Alternate 3 (Umbrella Roof) | Total w/ Alt 3 | Difference vs. Low Bid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **East Texas Industrial LLC** | $213,706 | $40,000 | $200,000 | **$453,706** | **Lowest Bidder** |
| **CROM CFG Industries** | $208,350 | $61,000 | $325,000 | **$594,350** | **+$140,644** |

### What the Recommendation Letter Omitted
* **No explanation of "best and most advantageous":** Pape-Dawson's [December 10 letter](/assets/docs/20251210_PDltr.pdf) states in a single sentence that [CROM CFG](https://www.cromcorp.com/) is recommended *"on the basis of being the best and most advantageous to the District,"* without citing a single reference check, technical deficiency, or disqualification against East Texas Industrial LLC.
* **No bid discrepancy flagged:** Pape-Dawson’s bid tabulation footnote specifically notes that bold/italicized names indicate mathematical errors or bid discrepancies. East Texas Industrial LLC was **not** flagged for any error.
* **A $125,000 markup on the roof alone:** While CROM CFG’s base bid was $5,356 lower, its price for Alternate No. 3 (the umbrella roof) was **$325,000** compared to East Texas Industrial’s **$200,000**—a 62.5% premium on that single line item.

### Even Worse: Inspecting a Tank *After* Awarding the Roof
The timeline of approvals in MUD No. 4’s 2026 minutes raises serious questions about engineering sequence and due diligence:
* **February 18, 2026:** On Jennifer Mays’ recommendation, [MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/)  unanimously approved CROM CFG’s **$325,000** umbrella roof replacement.
* **March 18, 2026:** One month *after* approving the $325,000 roof, Mays presented a **$14,800** proposal from Preventive Services to conduct an *Evaluation of Ground Storage Tank No. 1*, followed by an April revised proposal from [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) for **$65,068** (including $18,000 in direct expenses) to evaluate the very same tank.

### October 21, 2026: A Rare Pushback—Too Little, Too Late?
By **October 21, 2026**, the escalating costs finally hit a wall. At the MUD No. 4 board meeting, Pape-Dawson's representative (Jennifer Mays) requested approval of **Change Order No. 2 for $64,515** payable to [CROM CFG](https://www.cromcorp.com/) Industries for valve replacement at GST No. 1.

For the first time in years, a board member pushed back—moving to **decline** all action items in Pape-Dawson’s Engineer’s Report, including the $64,515 change order. Instead, the board capped the valve replacement project at **$45,000** under the direct supervision of MUD No. 4 board members Gesoff and Konyndyk, requiring their pre-approval for every expenditure.

While welcome, this isolated veto highlights a systemic failure: when directors actually scrutinize engineering proposals, **$19,515** is saved in a single vote. Had the same scrutiny been applied in December 2025 and February 2026, taxpayers might have saved hundreds of thousands more.
