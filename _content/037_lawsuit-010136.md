### The documents surrounding an outside law firm's engagement and a lawsuit against the Texas Attorney General
Grand Lakes residents have a simple interest in knowing who has authority to spend their district's money, hire outside counsel, and initiate litigation on behalf of a municipal utility district. Documents concerning [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) raise significant questions about those matters. This article details yet another episode of the MUD attorney's persistent mishandling of the MUD affairs.

### The May 20, 2024 engagement
An [engagement letter](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1k4mrYap686B3iJvO5coLovA1MqWqxREP/view) dated May 13, 2024 and signed on May 20, 2024 was used to engage the Austin law firm [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) in connection with our rate appeal to the Public Utility Commission of Texas ([PUCT](https://www.puc.texas.gov/)). The engagement letter bears the signature of Michael Christopher McClusky, then president of the MUD board. The authenticity of that signature is in doubt as we detailed in the article [The Paper Trail of an Invalid Rate Order](./035_LGLaw-engagement).

The significance of the engagement goes beyond the signature. The outside law firm subsequently became involved in further litigation concerning their own legal invoices to our MUD. While their engagement was, and should be, limited to MUD representation in our rate appeal (case no. [56589]((https://interchange.puc.texas.gov/Search/Filings?ControlNumber=56589))) before the [PUCT](https://www.puc.texas.gov/), they remain engaged over 2-1/2 years later.

### The Attorney General's ruling
Dated December 6, 2024, Texas Attorney General ruling **[OR-2024-040867](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1s8t0KcQdvODIKJLJdhvNqW4e9VAF59dj/view)** ordered the release of the [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) legal invoices to [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/). 

To block the release of said invoices, MUD attorney [Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) of [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) instructed [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) to sue the [Office of the Attorney General](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov), by illegally signing on December 23, 2024 a [supplemental contract](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1KWL26pgRcCTzcC6SBEBViFBOQEfTn1BR/view) to the illegal [May 13/20, 2024 contract](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1k4mrYap686B3iJvO5coLovA1MqWqxREP/view). 

Why illegally? The MUD is a local government, a political subdivision of the state, whose business can only be executed by its board in public, open meetings; never by its attorney fantasizing in his office, home, or car. The MUD attorney has absolutely no power or authority to sign any official document (contract, engagement letter, order, resolution, investment policy, insurance policy, etc.) on behalf of the MUD. As such, the December 23, 2024 [supplemental letter](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1KWL26pgRcCTzcC6SBEBViFBOQEfTn1BR/view) which enabled [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) to file suit no. [D-1-GN-24-010136](https://odysseyweb.traviscountytx.gov/app/RegisterOfActions/#/565B23C172A97821F23F1F46A3882F80DDE8728AEBE9CFD1EF96638D80E0350690EA0B93D4AE3EF05BDDB8D56BE52A9B523EA841C4A996602187153905F1FC896E8334F35A5A7584DD78B722E5373307) against the [Office of the Attorney General](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov) is absolutely illegal and invalid.

Worse is that [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) did not even wait for Skinner's illegal letter ([supplemental contract](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1KWL26pgRcCTzcC6SBEBViFBOQEfTn1BR/view)). They went ahead and filed the suit three days earlier, on December 20, 2024. The lawsuit D-1-GN-24-010136 remains active per the present. The Register of Actions is available [**here**](https://odysseyweb.traviscountytx.gov/app/RegisterOfActions/#/565B23C172A97821F23F1F46A3882F80DDE8728AEBE9CFD1EF96638D80E0350690EA0B93D4AE3EF05BDDB8D56BE52A9B523EA841C4A996602187153905F1FC896E8334F35A5A7584DD78B722E5373307).

### The documented sequence
Below we present a summary of the sequence of events:

**May 20, 2024:**
[Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) signs an [engagement letter](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1k4mrYap686B3iJvO5coLovA1MqWqxREP/view) for legal services from [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/). The MUD board president's signature on said letter is seemingly forged.

**December 6, 2024:**
The Texas Attorney General issues ruling [OR-2024-040867](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1s8t0KcQdvODIKJLJdhvNqW4e9VAF59dj/view), ordering the release of the [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) legal invoices to the MUD.

**December 20, 2024** and **December 23, 2024:**
[Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) files on December 20, 2024 a lawsuit against the Texas Attorney General based on a [supplemental engagement letter](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1KWL26pgRcCTzcC6SBEBViFBOQEfTn1BR/view) illegally signed by MUD attorney [Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) on December 23, 2024.

**June 15, 2026:**
[Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) brags about paying [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) **$216,365**. He further describes the law firm as one "*with extensive experience with state regulatory affairs*."

**Present issue:**
What board authorization existed for the preceding actions and expenditures? What is the $216,365 expense for? To prevent the residents from challenging a questionable rate order when the MUD had over $9 million in reserves? That is not an abstract question for Grand Lakes residents. It is public money.

### Why the invoices matter
Legal invoices can reveal what governmental entities are paying outside counsel to do. They can also provide residents with information about the legal matters for which their district is spending money.

When a governmental entity seeks to prevent disclosure of those invoices after the Attorney General has ordered their release, residents have a legitimate reason to ask why. The Portal is not asking readers to prejudge the litigation. We are asking them to examine the sequence of events.

### The basics of of governmental authority: What residents are entitled to ask
A municipal utility district is a governmental entity. Its residents therefore have a legitimate interest in posing questions and understanding:

* Who has authority to act for the district?
* Who can bind the district financially, contractually, and legally?
* Can the district's legal counsel act for the district? By which laws?
* Can the district's legal counsel bind the district financially, contractually, or legally? By which laws?
* Who authorized outside legal counsel?
* What action did the board take and when?
* What exactly did the May 20, 2024 engagement letter authorize?
* Was the signature on that document authentic?
* What was the outside legal counsel assignment?
* What marks the end of their assignment?
* What documents authorized the expenditure of district funds on outside legal services?
* What services did the outside legal counsel provide?
* What board action authorized the subsequent litigation?
* Why did the MUD seek to prevent release of legal invoices after the Attorney General's ruling?
* Did the board seek to prevent release of said invoices? Where is this minuted?
* What authority, if any, did the MUD attorney have to execute the December 23 Notice to Proceed, authorizing [Lloyd Gosselink Rochelle & Townsend](https://www.lglawfirm.com/) to sue the Texas Attorney General?
* Why was the lawsuit filed on December 20 if the Notice to Proceed was issued on December 23?
* How much did the [MUD](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) pay for the legal services of outside counsel from May 20, 2024 through the present?

These are questions about governance and public accountability that the MUD, its board, and its attorney are obliged to address.
