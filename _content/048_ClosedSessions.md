⚠️ 📋 In an October 6, 2026, meeting for the three Grand Lakes MUDs ([MUD4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/), [MUD1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/), [MUD2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/)) held at the office of the water company [Municipal District Services](https://mdswater.com/) (406 West Grand Parkway South, Suite 260, Katy, TX 77494), attorney [Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner.html) of [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) made 12 board members\[<span style="color:red;">*</span>\] enter into a 35-minute closed session under Texas Government Code § [551.071](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.551&artSec=551.071).

[<span style="color:red;">*</span>] [GLMUD4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/): Glenn Gesoff, John Schneller IV, Pete Konyndyk, Byron Broussard  
&nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; [GLMUD1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/): Omar Khan, Jonathan Reese, Paul Olendzki, Anand Maru  
&nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; [GLMUD2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/): John Scot Vann, William Donald Butler, Elizabeth Drinkard, George Armistead

Texas Government Code § [551.071](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.551&artSec=551.071) allows a closed session only when the governmental body seeks the advice of its attorney about pending or probable litigation or a settlement offer; or on a matter in which the attorney’s duty under the [Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct (TDRPC)](https://www.texasbar.com/AM/Template.cfm?Section=Home&ContentID=67638&Template=/CM/ContentDisplay.cfm) conflicts with the [Open Meetings Act](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.551&artSec=).

The posted [Notice](/assets/docs/20261006_GLMUD1-Agenda.pdf) of the October 6 public meeting did **not** include specific agenda items detailing the exact litigation or legal issues the board members needed to discuss with the attorney in the closed session. And the [minutes](/assets/docs/20261006_GLMUD1-MM.pdf) of said meeting do not tell whatsoever what was discussed in the closed session. These are violations of Government Code §§ [551.144](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.551&artSec=551.144) (jail time applicable) and [551.145](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.551&artSec=551.145). 

**Texas Government Code ~ Chapter 551 (Open Meetings Act)**<br><br>
Sec. 551.144. CLOSED MEETING;  OFFENSE;  PENALTY.  
(a) A member of a governmental body commits an offense if a closed meeting is not permitted under this chapter and the member knowingly:  
 &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;  1. calls or aids in calling or organizing the closed meeting, whether it is a special or called closed meeting;  
 &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;  2. closes or aids in closing the meeting to the public, if it is a regular meeting; or  
 &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp;  3. participates in the closed meeting, whether it is a regular, special, or called meeting.  
(b) An offense under Subsection (a) is a misdemeanor punishable by:  
 &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; 1. a fine of not less than $100 or more than $500;  
 &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; 2. confinement in the county jail for not less than one month or more than six months; or  
 &nbsp; &nbsp; &nbsp; 3. both the fine and confinement.  
(c) It is an affirmative defense to prosecution under Subsection (a) that the member of the governmental body acted in reasonable reliance on a court order or a written interpretation of this chapter contained in an opinion of a court of record, the attorney general, or the attorney for the governmental body.<br><br>
Sec. 551.145. CLOSED MEETING WITHOUT CERTIFIED AGENDA OR RECORDING; OFFENSE; PENALTY.  
(a) A member of a governmental body commits an offense if the member participates in a closed meeting of the governmental body knowing that a certified agenda of the closed meeting is not being kept or that a recording of the closed meeting is not being made.  
(b) An offense under Subsection (a) is a Class C misdemeanor.
{: .flash2}

The [Texas Ethics Commission](https://www.ethics.texas.gov/) is a role model when it comes to proper and lawful handling of executive (closed) sessions. They produce specific and detailed agenda and minutes so that the public is aware of the litigation and/or legal matters that warrant deliberations in a closed session. As a sample, one can check the [Agenda](https://www.ethics.state.tx.us/data/meetings/agendas/2025/09-17-2025_Executive-Agenda.pdf) and [Minutes](https://www.ethics.state.tx.us/data/meetings/minutes/2025/09-17-2025_Minutes_ExecSession.pdf) for the Executive Session of their Public Meeting of September 17, 2025.

Under Texas Government Code § [551.141](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.551&artSec=551.141), any formal actions or votes subsequently authorized in open session that stem from these illegally noticed and improperly closed deliberations are legally voidable and can be struck down in court. 

📌 The entire Grand Lakes community, comprising 2,740 households and 40 businesses, is impacted by the violations. An investigation by the [Fort Bend County District Attorney's Office](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/district-attorney) is warranted.
