The Grand Lakes story is ultimately not about one attorney, one engineer, one operator, one bookkeeping firm, or one disputed document. It is about **public governance**.

It is about whether a community can entrust hundreds of millions of dollars of long-term infrastructure, taxes, utility payments and public expenditures to governmental institutions without meaningful independent oversight.

It is about whether residents can obtain information about their own government.

It is about whether elected and appointed officials understand that they work for the public.

It is about whether professional consultants remember that they work for the governmental entities that hire and pay them.

And it is about whether laws governing public bodies actually mean something when residents invoke them.

Serving the Grand Lakes water districts are a number of consultants who have rendered their services since 1986 when Grand Lakes was known as Via Ranch. The long-standing tenure of said consultants has resulted in severe institutional entrenchment, operational practices characterized by professional indifference, a pattern of systemic non-compliance, disregard for the law, and a documented breach of fiduciary duty. The result could not be more grave—creating an adversarial posture toward necessary oversight and board governance, exposing the district to significant legal liability, and failing to protect the public interest of the water districts.

The Grand Lakes neighborhood encompasses 2,740 households and 40 local businesses. Each and every constituent of those is affected by the irregularities we just alerted to—disregard for the law, a "trust us" attitude when the acts and practices point in a different direction, and much more. Problems have lasted too long. The financial impact is considerable. There are serious questions concerning consultant conduct. There are serious questions concerning board oversight. The need for future deterrence and accountability is urgent and important. These are precisely the issues a democratic society should be willing to examine.
