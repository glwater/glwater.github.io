[Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) has administered the Grand Lakes water districts since 1986. One of the serious issues committed by their staff concerns tampering with, and forgery of signatures on, governmental documents, including—but not limited to—board meeting minutes, resolutions, orders, tax levies, investment policies, insurance policies, and engagement letters. Christopher Todd Skinner, Schwartz, Page & Harding's lead attorney following the Grand Lakes water districts since 2003, cannot invoke confusion, negligence, or ignorance. He, of course, maintains silence since confronted with the matter. He will not police himself. He sits and waits for the competent state agencies and authorities to take action.

A chronological collection of documents from 2021 through 2026 compare signatures attributed to [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) board members—board president Michael Christopher McClusky and board secretary Gregory Jay Henry—with baseline signatures from their campaign finance filings. Said documents contain apparent unauthorized signatures, forged signatures, and digital anomalies. This is extraordinarily serious, such that intervention by the authorities is required. Texas Penal Code addresses signature forgery in § [32.21](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=PE&chapter=PE.32&artSec=32.21) and tampering with governmental records in § [37.10](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=PE&chapter=PE.37&artSec=37.10).

Because this is serious, it should **not** be decided by residents, consultants, or political factions. It should be independently investigated by appropriate authorities ([Harris County District Attorney's Office](https://dao.harriscountytx.gov/), [FBI Houston](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/houston), [Texas Attorney General](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/), [Texas Commission on Environmental Quality](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/), and others) and, where necessary, qualified forensic document examiners. If the signatures are authentic, that should be established. If they are not authentic, that should also be established. If governmental records were altered, improperly executed, or submitted with false information, the responsible parties should be identified and held accountable. The community deserves an answer based on evidence—not competing assertions or baseless defenses from those who will cover up their wrongdoings.
