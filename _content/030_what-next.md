Grand Lakes does not need another argument between residents and consultants. It needs facts. We therefore believe the following steps deserve consideration:

#### 1. Conduct an independent forensic financial examination.
The review should cover expenditures, contracts, legal fees, engineering fees, operator charges, bookkeeping, tax collection, banking, investments, capital projects, and related-party or potentially conflicted transactions.

#### 2. Conduct an independent examination of governmental records.
This should include questioned signatures, resolutions, orders, minutes, affidavits, contracts, and other documents identified in the underlying report.

#### 3. Conduct a comprehensive Public Information Act review.
Residents should not have to repeatedly fight for access to records that the law makes available to the public.

#### 4. Review every major consultant contract.
The districts should determine whether existing contracts remain necessary, competitive, and in the community's best interest.

#### 5. Establish genuine internal controls.
Financial reporting, investments, disbursements, contracts, capital assets, and consultant performance should be subject to independent checks.

#### 6. Require independent review of major projects and proposed debt.
Before another major bond proposal is placed before voters, residents should receive complete documentation demonstrating need, cost, alternatives, ownership, projected operating costs, and the identity of the professionals who stand to be paid.

#### 7. Examine consolidation of the four districts.
The community should receive an objective study of the financial, operational, and legal consequences of consolidating the four Grand Lakes water districts ([MUD4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com), [MUD1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com), [MUD2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com), and [WCID](https://districtdirectory.org/fort-bend-county/grand-lakes-water-control-and-improvement-district/)).

#### 8. Examine the City's options under the Strategic Partnership Agreements.
Where the City has audit or other contractual rights, those rights should be exercised when circumstances warrant.

#### 9. Ask state authorities to investigate credible evidence of wrongdoing.
The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality ([TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/)), Texas Attorney General ([OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/)), [Secretary of State](https://www.sos.texas.gov/), [Fort Bend County District Attorney's Office](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/district-attorney), [Harris County District Attorney's Office](https://dao.harriscountytx.gov/), [Houston Police Department](https://www.houstontx.gov/police/), Texas Department of Public Safety \| [Texas Rangers](https://www.dps.texas.gov/section/texas-rangers), [FBI Houston](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/houston), and other competent law-enforcement authorities and governmental bodies should independently evaluate matters falling within their respective jurisdictions.

#### 10. Restore the principle that the boards—not their consultants—govern the districts.
Consultants work for the public entities. They do not own them.
