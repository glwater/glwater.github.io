Grand Lakes is a mature community in Katy, Fort Bend County, Texas. What began as Via Ranch in 1986 has grown into a community of approximately 2,740 residences and 40 businesses.

Today, the community is served by four separate governmental water districts: Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District Nos. 1, 2 and 4, and the Grand Lakes Water Control and Improvement District (WCID). Together, the districts occupy 1,390 acres.

For many residents, these districts are simply part of the background of daily life. We pay our water and wastewater bills. We pay property taxes. We expect our streets, drainage, water and wastewater systems, and other public infrastructure to be properly maintained. We expect elected or appointed public officials to supervise the professionals they hire. And we expect public records and public money to be handled according to law.

But an examination of district records raises a much more fundamental question: **Who is actually governing the Grand Lakes water districts—the boards elected or appointed to govern them, or the private consultants hired to serve them?**

The documentation assembled by Grand Lakes residents raises serious questions about the operation of the districts, the conduct of consultants, the integrity of certain governmental records, the handling of public information, the adequacy of financial controls, the justification for major expenditures and proposed debt, and the degree to which residents have been given meaningful oversight of the institutions that spend their money.

These matters deserve independent examination—not dismissal, concealment, or another round of business as usual.
