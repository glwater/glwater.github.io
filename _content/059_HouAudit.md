For more than a decade, an extraordinary stream of sales tax money flowed into Grand Lakes—and into the City of Houston's treasury—under the 30-year Strategic Partnership Agreements (SPAs) executed in 2005. Now that the windfall has ended and district cash reserves are being burned at an alarming pace, the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) has both the contractual right and the public duty to step in with an immediate forensic audit across **Grand Lakes [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/), [MUD No. 1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/), and [MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/)**.

### The $27.3 Million Windfall—and What Happened Next
Under Section 5.02 of the Strategic Partnership Agreement (effective March 15, 2005 for [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/)), a 1% [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) sales and use tax is split 50/50 between the City and the District. Between FY2013 and FY2025, each party received **$27,310,173** in SPA revenue—roughly **$22.8 million** of which resulted from accounting and reporting errors at [The Home Depot](https://www.homedepot.com/) store at 6850 S Fry Rd, Katy, TX 77494 (see our earlier investigation: [*The $27.3M Home Depot Windfall: What Happened to Grand Lakes MUD No. 2's Free Money?*](./047_HD)).

Once [The Home Depot](https://www.homedepot.com/) corrected its reporting, annual SPA revenue plunged from a peak of **$5.16 million** in FY2022 down to **$339,369** in FY2025. Yet instead of scaling back spending to match reality, district consultants kept the spending machine running at full throttle:

* **Rapid Reserve Depletion:** MUD No. 2’s reserves peaked at **$9,134,604** in FY2023, dropped to **$5,963,471** in FY2025, and fell further to **$4,352,960** by September 2026—a burn rate of **$1.3 million to $1.9 million per year**.
* **Wildly Disproportionate Spending:** Although [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) is completely debt-free and only **64% to 70% the size** of [MUD No. 1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/) and [MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/), it has been operated with **up to 46% higher expenditures** than its neighboring districts.
* **Consultant Fee Escalation:** Annual increases in legal, operational, and consulting fees have far outpaced inflation, CPI, and normal municipal benchmarks—despite Grand Lakes being a fully built-out community with zero new residential development.

### The City’s Contractual Audit Mandate (SPA Sections 5.04 & 5.06)
The [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) does not need to wait for a crisis or a new bond election to examine how these millions were spent. The Strategic Partnership Agreement explicitly grants the City direct audit authority:

* **Section 5.04 (Restricted Use of Funds):** Requires the District to use its 50% share of SPA sales and use tax revenue *only* for purposes for which the District is lawfully authorized to use ad valorem tax or other public revenues.
* **Section 5.06 (City Audit Rights):** Expressly empowers the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) to audit the District’s expenditures made with Section 5.02 sales and use tax revenue to verify strict compliance with Section 5.04.

Because all three Grand Lakes districts—[MUD No. 1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/), [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/), and [MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/)—share overlapping consultants (led by general counsel [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/)), joint facilities, and multi-district security contracts exceeding $1.1 million annually, auditing [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) in isolation is not enough. A coordinated audit across **all three MUDs** is required to trace inter-district cost shifting and shared vendor billings.

### Material Breach & Remedies Under Article VIII
Under **Section 8.01 of the SPA**, a district commits a Material Breach when it uses SPA funds for unauthorized purposes, fails to operate and maintain utility and drainage infrastructure in compliance with Article VI, or fails to act in good faith to protect residents and lower their tax burden.

When a material breach is identified, the SPA obligates the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) to:
* **Determine** whether the District failed to comply with the SPA.
* **Determine** whether any non-compliance is excusable.
* **Establish** a binding method and timeline for the District to cure the failure.

If the failure is unexcused, **Section 8.04(A)** authorizes the City to pursue full legal and equitable remedies—including **monetary awards** and **termination of the SPA**. Furthermore, while Section 7.01 restricts unilateral full-purpose annexation during the 30-year term, **Texas Local Government Code § 43.0751(h)** confirms that the City may terminate the SPA and initiate annexation proceedings *at any time prior to March 2035* upon request of the District’s governing body.

### What Houston Officials Should Demand Now
* **Invoke SPA Section 5.06 immediately** across Grand Lakes [MUD No. 1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/), [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/), and [MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/) for FY2013 through FY2026.
* **Trace every dollar of the $27.3M SPA windfall**—specifically examining consultant fee spikes, multi-district security contracts, and capital outlays that drained reserves from $9.13M to $4.35M.
* **Reject any attempt to extend the March 2035 SPA expiration** or issue unnecessary long-term bond debt that would saddle Houston taxpayers and Grand Lakes residents with decades of avoidable liabilities.
