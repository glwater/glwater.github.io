The three Grand Lakes MUDs ([MUD No. 1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/), [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/), [MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/)) have an unusual relationship with the City of Houston. The Strategic Partnership Agreement (SPA) each of the three MUDs has with the City of Houston provides for sales-tax revenue to be shared between the City and the district and that the agreement contains provisions concerning the lawful use of that revenue and City audit rights.

The circumstances warrant a thorough City audit. This request deserves serious consideration. If a governmental entity receives public revenue under a formal agreement that expressly provides audit rights, then an independent examination of whether those revenues have been used properly is entirely consistent with the principle of public accountability.

The SPA identifies statutory provisions concerning termination of the agreement and possible annexation before the agreement's full-purpose annexation conversion date. Given the remarkable manner in which the [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) attorneys have administered the four Grand Lakes water districts for years, the community should therefore ask:

**Would consolidation, restructuring, or eventual annexation produce a more transparent, economical and accountable system of public utility governance?**

These are legitimate public-policy questions. They deserve an objective answer.
