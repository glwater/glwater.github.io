Across Grand Lakes MUDs 1, 2, 4, and the WCID, 2,740 households and 40 businesses bear the financial consequences of runaway consultant fees, multi-million-dollar reserve depletion, and unchallenged vendor contracts. When residents—including dozens of scientists, PhDs, engineers, and financial professionals—present documented audit data and ask basic governance questions, entrenched directors (MUD/WCID board members) respond with dismissal, closed-door executive sessions, and continued rubber-stamping.

We cannot—and will not—respond to tens of millions of dollars in documented waste with polite applause. **Public office is a position of public trust, not a ceremonial title.**

### 1. Seasoned Professionals Cannot Feign Confusion
When water-district expenditures go off the rails, the standard defense offered by board members is *"We relied on our attorney,"* *"Our bookkeeper prepared the numbers,"* or *"We didn't realize."* 

In Grand Lakes, that excuse collapses on contact with reality. The individuals sitting on our four water-district boards (listed in our [Board Directory](https://glwater.github.io/guides/009_GLWD-boards.html)) are not novices or uninitiated volunteers—they are accomplished professionals whose daily careers require mastery of contracts, compliance, budgets, and risk management:

* **Legal Practitioners:** Such as **John Schneller IV** (MUD 4), a practicing attorney and law firm owner in Houston.
* **Financial professionals:** Such as **William Donald Butler** (MUD 2), a relationship banker at [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/) in Houston.
* **Engineering Executives & Senior Engineers:** Such as **John Scot Vann, P.E.** (MUD 2), President & CEO of [Edge Engineering & Science](https://edge-es.com/); **Matthew Froehlich** (WCID), engineer at [BGE Inc.](https://www.bgeinc.com/); and **Richard Brownlee** (WCID), engineer at [NOV](https://www.nov.com/).
* **Corporate & Energy Industry Veterans:** Such as **Glenn Gesoff** (MUD 4) at [BP](https://www.bp.com/), and **George Armistead** (MUD 2), a retired oil & gas drilling professional.
* **Business Owners & Managers:** Such as **Gregory Jay Henry** (MUD 2), owner of [XperNet Services](http://www.xpernet.com/) and [KPM Management](https://kpmcommunities.com/).

Every single one of these directors knows how to read a balance sheet, question a vendor invoice, spot a conflict of interest, and demand competitive bidding in their private-sector work. Expecting the community to believe they suddenly lost those basic skills when governing public money insults the intelligence of every resident in Grand Lakes.

### 2. Decades on the Board—Zero Excuse for Fiduciary Failure
Even setting aside their professional credentials, tenure eliminates any claim of a "learning curve":

* Directors such as **Glenn Gesoff**, **John Scot Vann**, and **Gregory Jay Henry** have sat on Grand Lakes water-district boards for **over 12 years**.
* Former MUD 2 director **Michael Christopher McClusky** held a board seat for **24 years** (from 2002 until voters removed him in May 2026).

How does a director serve for 12 to 24 years without learning the fundamental statutory, ethical, and fiduciary duties of a Texas water-district officer? As detailed in our guide on [The Legal, Ethical, Moral, and Civic Duties of Water-District Boards](https://glwater.github.io/guides/003_Duties-Boards.html), hiring outside lawyers, bookkeepers, or operators **never** authorizes a board to surrender its independent governmental judgment.

### 3. The "Reasonable Citizen" Standard and the Trap of Blind Reliance
Directors who blindly sign whatever is placed in front of them by conflicted consultants are walking into a legal and ethical trap. 

Under Texas law, public officials are fiduciaries of taxpayer funds. When the [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/), the [State Auditor's Office](https://sao.texas.gov/), or public integrity investigators examine why millions of dollars in reserves evaporated—or why multi-district contracts were approved without independent scrutiny—the very consultants who profited from those decisions will not take the fall for the board.

Instead, the predictable defense of every outside advisor is simple: *"We only recommended; the Board voted and approved."* And the standard applied to directors is equally unforgiving: **Did you exercise even the minimum level of judgment and diligence that a reasonable, ordinary citizen would exercise with their own affairs?**

When seasoned engineers, attorneys, and business owners fail to meet even the basic standard of an ordinary citizen, it ceases to look like mere negligence—it reflects a profound breach of public honor and institutional capture.

### 4. Accountability Is Underway
The era of unmonitored board meetings in Grand Lakes is over:

* **At the Ballot Box:** In May 2026, Grand Lakes voters swept out every incumbent who stood for re-election across the MUDs. The next MUD board elections in May 2028 will finish what the community started.
* **Through Public Scrutiny:** Seventy-seven investigative articles on this portal now document the financial records, closed-door meetings, and vendor contracts across all four Grand Lakes water districts.
* **Through State & Municipal Oversight:** Formal audit demands and regulatory referrals have been submitted to the [Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts](https://comptroller.texas.gov/), the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/), and state oversight authorities.

📌 Directors still sitting on these boards face a clear choice: **restore independent oversight, halt wasteful spending, and open the books to the public—or own the full legal, financial, and reputational consequences of rubber-stamping the depletion of our community.**
