[Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) legal counsel, [**Christopher Todd Skinner**](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) of [**Schwartz, Page & Harding**](https://www.sphllp.com/), has demonstrated a persistent pattern of non-compliance with the [Texas Public Information Act (Government Code Chapter 552)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=). Despite eight separate orders from the [Office of the Attorney General (OAG)](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) in May 2026 and two mandatory retraining orders in October 2026, he continues delays and procedural barriers to obstruct resident access to public records.

### The Eight May 11, 2026 OAG Rulings
On May 11, 2026, the [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) issued eight separate complaint determinations against [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) after its attorney, [Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner), withheld public records requested as far back as June 2025: **OR-25-044591-IC**, **OR-25-051081-IC**, **OR-25-057685-IC**, **OR-26-000106-IC**, **OR-26-003417-IC**, **OR-26-004049-IC**, **OR-26-015987-IC**, **OR-26-023144-IC**.

In every single determination, the [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) cited Texas Government Code § [552.221(a)–(b)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.221), reminding that a governmental body must *"promptly produce public information"* and that *"the manner in which the information is provided is up to the requestor, not the governmental body."* The [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) ordered the district to promptly produce the requested records in the manner requested or seek an official ruling to withhold them.

### Post-Ruling Obstruction: 39-Day Delays and $1,800 in Cost Estimates
Rather than complying promptly with the May 11 orders, [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) deployed new roadblocks:

* **A 39-Day Delay:** [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) took 39 days to release only a portion of the records the [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) had ordered produced—exceeding even the 30-day outer statutory window under Texas Government Code § [552.306(c)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.306).
* **Punitive Cost Estimates:** On June 2, 2026, [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) issued two itemized cost estimates—32 hours ($576) and 68 hours ($1,224)—totaling **$1,800** in an effort to price residents out of accessing public records.
* **Recycled Withholding Requests:** [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) filed new requests for [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) rulings attempting to withhold portions of the very information the [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) had already ordered released on May 11.

### Escalation: The October 22, 2026 Mandatory Training Orders
The pattern of non-compliance led to escalated [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) intervention five months later. On October 22, 2026, in two separate enforcement letters (**OR-25-057172-IC** and **OR-26-007846-IC**), the [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) formally found that [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) violated Government Code § [552.221](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.221) by failing to produce information promptly or certify a release date and hour within ten business days.

Pursuant to Texas Government Code § [552.328(c)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.328), the [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) imposed two sanctions on the district:
* **Fee Forfeiture & Refunds (§ [552.328(c)(2)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.328)):** The district is barred from assessing any costs to the requestor for the records at issue and must refund any fees already collected.
* **Mandatory Open Records Training (§ [552.328(c)(1)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.328)):** The district’s public information officer or designee was ordered to complete official Open Records Training and submit a certificate of completion within six months (by April 22, 2027).

### Ongoing Violations of the Public Information Act
Even after ten adverse [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) rulings across May and October 2026, residents continue to face systemic procedural abuse:

* **Treating "10 Business Days" as a Minimum Floor:** Since June 2025, the [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) attorneys have answered every single public information request submitted to the district after 14 or more calendar days. Under § [552.221(a)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.221), *"promptly"* means *"as soon as possible under the circumstances, that is, within a reasonable time, without delay."* The 10-business-day rule under § [552.221(d)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.221) applies only when a governmental body genuinely cannot produce records within that timeframe and certifies a specific date and hour for release—it is not a license to sit on routine electronic records for two weeks.
* **Inventing a "5:00 PM Cutoff" Double Standard:** [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) has repeatedly treated electronic public information requests submitted after 5:00 PM as received on the next business day, while simultaneously sending district responses after 5:00 PM on or after the tenth business day. Under standard electronic filing rules, electronic requests received by 11:59 PM are received on that calendar day—unlike physical mail delivered after business hours when staff have left the office.

### Why This Matters to Grand Lakes Residents
Every hour [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) spends challenging public information requests, drafting cost estimates, requesting [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) rulings, fighting [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) rulings, and taking mandatory remedial training is billed to Grand Lakes water district taxpayers. Public records belong to the residents who fund the district—not the law firm hired to administer it.
