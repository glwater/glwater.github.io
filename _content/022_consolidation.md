Another structural question deserves attention.

Grand Lakes occupies a relatively small geographic area (1,390 acres or 2.17 sq. miles), yet the community is divided among four separate governmental districts.

[Chapter 54](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.54&artSec=) of the Texas Water Code provides mechanisms for consolidation of districts, including provisions addressing consolidation, elections, governance, debts, taxes, and unissued bonds.

<blockquote markdown="1">
[Chapter 54](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.54&artSec=) of the Texas Water Code.  
Subchapter H. Adding and Excluding Territory; Consolidating and Dissolving Districts.  
§ [54.728](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.54&artSec=54.728) | Consolidation of Districts.  
§ [54.729](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.54&artSec=54.729) | Elections to Approve Consolidation.  
§ [54.730](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.54&artSec=54.730) | Governing Consolidated Districts.  
§ [54.731](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.54&artSec=54.731) | Debts of Original Districts.  
§ [54.732](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.54&artSec=54.732) | Assessment and Collection of Taxes.  
§ [54.733](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.54&artSec=54.733) | Voted but Unissued Bonds.
</blockquote>

Consolidation would not automatically solve every problem. But it is reasonable to ask whether four separate administrative structures are necessary for a single established community over 1,390 acres (2.17 sq mi)—or whether they create duplicated costs, duplicated professional services, duplicated contracts, duplicated governance, and additional opportunities for fragmentation.

The community should have a fact-based study answering that question.
