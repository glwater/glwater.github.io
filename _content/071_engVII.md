Internal [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) budget calculation sheets included in the 2026 Grand Lakes board packets pull back the curtain on how engineering fees routinely balloon from routine maintenance into tens of thousands of dollars in billings.

### 1. Marking Up Mandatory TCEQ Fees & Newspaper Ads by 10%
In April 2026, [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) submitted a proposal to [Grand Lakes MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/) to renew the Wastewater Treatment Plant’s **Texas Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (TPDES) Permit** (Task 237).
* In the [April 2026 MUD 4 Minutes](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/static/815a2a438d461b249985908fadb25bd3/Minutes_2026_22_22_Approved_9864_d9634bd214.pdf), two overlapping items appeared: **$43,000** for the *Wastewater Treatment Plant Discharge Permit Renewal Application* and **$25,000** for the *Pollutant Discharge Elimination System Permit renewal*—two names for the exact same TPDES permit expiring May 13, 2027.
* Pape-Dawson’s internal **Level of Effort and Expenses Estimate** reveals why the cost jumped from $25,000 to $44,565:<br><br>
  * **High Hourly Rates:** Billing **$315/hour** for Senior Project Manager, **$270/hour** for Principal Engineer, **$210/hour** for Designer, **$185/hour** for Project Coordinator, and **$180/hour** for an Entry-Level Engineer-in-Training (E.I.T.)—including 66 hours of E.I.T. time ($11,880) and 10 hours of Principal Engineer time ($2,700) just to fill out standard [TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/) renewal forms.
  * **10% Markup on Public Notices & State Fees:** [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) took **$17,843** in pass-through expenses—including the **$1,615 [TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/) permit fee** and **$16,107 in mandatory English/Spanish newspaper notices** (*Houston Chronicle* and *La Voz*)—and tacked on a **10% markup** (billing MUD4 **$1,777** for the [TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/) fee and **$19,945** total).

### 2. Charging for "Additional Services" on Post-90% Redesigns
On the **SH 99 Reclaimed Water Line Relocation**, [TxDOT](https://www.txdot.gov/) had agreed to joint-bid the utility relocation as part of its frontage road project. Yet on **April 22, 2026**, Jennifer Mays submitted **Additional Services Request No. 02** for **$12,000** (52 hours of staff time) to adjust utility offsets after TxDOT’s 90% review. When [TxDOT](https://www.txdot.gov/) refused to pay for the redesign, Jennifer Mays and MUD4 passed the $12,000 bill straight to Grand Lakes taxpayers (over Director Konyndyk’s lone opposition vote)—despite contract exclusions disclaiming boundary surveys, structural design, traffic control plans, and agency review fees.

### 3. Inflated Engineer Estimates vs. Real Market Bids
On the **Peek Road and South Fry Road Water Line Replacement**, Pape-Dawson’s Engineer’s Opinion of Probable Construction Cost (OPC) projected **$540,810.00** ($471,610 base + $69,200 paving). When sealed bids opened on **March 24, 2026**, **every single contractor bid far below Pape-Dawson’s estimate**. Yet [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) still billed **$58,100** for Construction-Phase Services (including **$19,500** for 100 hours of "periodic field observation" at $195/hr and **$11,000** for material testing)—equal to **18.9% of the entire construction contract**—and approved **Change Order No. 1 ($14,939)** for two additional isolation valves that were missed in the original design.
* **ARS Construction LLC (Low Bid):** **$307,725.00** (43% below Pape-Dawson’s OPC).
* **On Par Civil Services:** **$339,997.79**.
* **BDGpro:** **$341,204**.
* **Bull-G Construction LLC:** **$422,055**.

### 4. Questions for the Texas Board of Professional Engineers (TBPELS)
Under **22 TAC § [137.57](https://texas-sos.appianportalsgov.com/rules-and-meetings?$locale=en_US&interface=VIEW_TAC_SUMMARY&queryAsDate=11%2F26%2F2026&recordId=209329)** and **§ [137.63](https://texas-sos.appianportalsgov.com/rules-and-meetings?$locale=en_US&interface=VIEW_TAC_SUMMARY&queryAsDate=11%2F26%2F2026&recordId=202230)**, Texas Professional Engineers are required to act as faithful agents and trustees for public employers, avoid misleading representations, and exercise due care when affixing their professional seal. The documented record in Grand Lakes raises formal regulatory questions regarding:
- Affixing a P.E. seal to a Bond Authorization Report containing a **$10,000,000 subtotal math error**, conflicting reimbursement figures ($90k vs. $190k vs. $187k), and conflicting ownership percentages.
- Billing 10% subconsultant markups on contracts whose own exclusions state those services should be contracted directly *"to avoid engineer markup"*.
- Recommending a construction bid **$140,644 above the lowest responsive bidder** (CROM CFG vs. East Texas Industrial on GST No. 1) without written technical justification.
- Unreasonably inflated engineer estimates versus real market bids.
- Excessive hourly rates and overbilling that do not match the scope or complexity of the work.
