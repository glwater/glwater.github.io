<blockquote style="border-left: 5px solid #0066cc;" markdown="1">
Members of the boards of the Grand Lakes Water Districts (MUD4, MUD1, MUD2, and WCID):

We are writing to urge immediate, coordinated fiduciary oversight of our water districts' proposals, contracts, cost-sharing splits, and contractor selection.

Over the past year, residents have audited the official board minutes, engineering proposals, bid tabulations, and bond reports submitted by [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) and reviewed by [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/). What those public records show is a disturbing pattern of taxpayers being overcharged while basic due diligence is skipped.

### Our binding pro-rata cost splits are being ignored
Under our regional agreements, [MUD2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) owns 21.08% of the wastewater plant, [MUD1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/) owns 29.10%, and [MUD4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/) owns 49.82%. Yet in January and February of this year, [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) pitched separate proposals to each board for Lift Station No. 1 engineering—totaling $384,821—and split it almost equally, overcharging [MUD2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) by nearly $44,000 on engineering alone. Worse, in MUD2’s September and October Bond Summary of Costs, 100% of Lift Station No. 1 construction—$990,376—is dumped onto [MUD2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) alone, while WWTP projects are billed at 33.66% instead of 21.08%. Meanwhile, on the Peek and Fry Road waterline, [MUD2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) was stuck paying 50% based on a verbal claim from our attorney—without a single sealed boundary survey showing pipe footage.

### Competitive bidding and contract rules are not being enforced
On Ground Storage Tank No. 1, [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) recommended—and [MUD4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/) approved—a $594,350 contract to CROM CFG that was $140,644 higher than the lowest error-free bidder, East Texas Industrial, with zero written justification in the award letter. On Lift Station No. 1, [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/)  charged a 10% markup on coating inspection and material testing, even though Page 3 of Pape-Dawson’s own contract says those services should be contracted directly by the District "to avoid engineer markup." And on the Texas Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (TPDES) permit renewal, [Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) even marked up mandatory [TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/) state filing fees and newspaper ads by 10%.

### The $27.93 million Bond Authorization Report sealed in February contains glaring errors
Table 1 of the February 2, 2026 Bond Authorization Report has a $10 million error in the sewer subtotal. The Jason’s Deli waterline reimbursement is listed at $90,000 in one table, $190,000 two pages earlier, and $187,336 in the bond summary. And Exhibit A schedules millions of dollars to recoat tanks that Preventive Services’ own inspection said required "no corrective action." While we defeated this bond authorization in the May 2026 election, the negligence is troubling and requires your urgent attention.

### Board scrutiny must become the standard
For years, Pape-Dawson's representatives have repeatedly followed low initial budgets with supplemental fee requests and change orders—effectively rendering the competitive bidding process meaningless once a contractor is selected. At MUD4’s [October 21, 2026 meeting](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/static/72d0556b774bc0fa17321208b02d78c2/Minutes_2026_10_21_approved_9864_9cbf50143c.pdf), board member Gesoff finally pushed back—rejecting a $64,515 change order and capping the work at $45,000. That proves that when the boards scrutinize consultants, taxpayers save money. After years of costly change orders, this level of oversight must become the standard across all four Grand Lakes water districts, not the exception.

📌 We ask the Grand Lakes water district boards to take the following immediate actions:

- Freeze all bond applications and new engineering task orders until an independent third-party engineer audits Pape-Dawson’s CIP and cost-split tables.
- Require a unified, cross-district cost table on every shared project showing total combined costs and exact contractual percentages.
- Demand written justification before ever bypassing a low bidder or approving any change order.
- Effectuate district-wide consultant procurement and fee controls. <br><br>
    - Mandatory RFP Cycles: Require competitive Requests for Proposals (RFPs) or Requests for Qualifications (RFQs) every 2 years for all recurring vendors (general counsel, operator, engineer, bookkeeper, tax collector, financial advisor, auditor), prohibiting evergreen contracts.
    - Itemized Billing & Retainer Audits: Eliminate vague monthly retainers or "general administration" pass-through charges. Require line-item invoicing with time-entry logs and pre-approval caps for non-routine work across all consultants.
    - Benchmarking Multi-District Rates: Mandate an annual comparison of consultant fee structures, operator base fees, and legal hourly rates against neighboring MUDs of similar size and infrastructure age.
    - Subcontractor Markup Caps: Prohibit operators and attorneys from applying unapproved percentage markups on third-party subcontracts, lab testing, or filing fees.

Thank you for your time and service to our community—a community asking who is accountable for its water districts!
</blockquote>
