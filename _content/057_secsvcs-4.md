We expand on [Part I](./054_secsvcs-1), [Part II](./055_secsvcs-2), and [Part III](./056_secsvcs-3) to show in this Part how a deeply conflicted MUD attorney plays the Grand Lakes water district boards off one another for "just one more year," with the unmistakable goal of systematically draining cash reserves ahead of the 2034–2035 [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) Strategic Partnership Agreement (SPA) and annexation window.

When elected water district directors finally question why taxpayers are spending over $1.1 million a year on "additional" Sheriff and Constable patrol contracts—and propose cutting those costs—who steps in to keep the spending pipeline open for "just one more year"? Not the Sheriff. Not the Constable. It is the Grand Lakes water districts' own general counsel, **[Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner)** of **[Schwartz, Page & Harding (SPH)](https://www.sphllp.com/)**.

A side-by-side comparison of official board minutes from **[July 2024](/assets/docs/20240715_GLMUD2-MM.pdf)** and **[August 2026](/assets/docs/20260819_GLMUD4-MM.pdf)** reveals a repeatable playbook used by [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) to neutralize cost-cutting proposals across Grand Lakes [MUD No. 1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/), [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/), and [MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/), isolate any board questioning the expense, invoke the other two districts to create artificial peer pressure, and push any reduction off to "a later time" or the next contract cycle.

### The Playbook in Action: Two Years Apart, Same Script

#### 1. July 15, 2024 — Neutralizing Cuts at Grand Lakes MUD No. 2
At the [July 15, 2024 Grand Lakes MUD No. 2 Board Meeting](/assets/docs/20240715_GLMUD2-MM.pdf), MUD board president Michael Christopher McClusky put concrete cost-saving proposals on the table:
* Eliminate funding for the Fort Bend County Sheriff’s Office (FBCSO) deputies contracting through the Grand Lakes Community Association (GLCA) for the 2024–2025 term.
* Cut the District’s Fort Bend County Precinct 1 Constable’s Office (FBCCO) deputy count in half—from **four (4) deputies down to two (2)**.

Rather than advising the Board on its fiduciary duty to evaluate whether taxpayers were getting fair market value, [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) immediately leveraged [MUD No. 1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/) to stall the reform:

> *"Mr. Skinner reported that the Board of Directors of Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District No. 4 ('MUD No. 4') will consider this request at its July Board meeting, and the Board of Directors of Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District No. 1 ('MUD No. 1') has considered the request, and voted to continue to fund the FBCSO's Contract with the GLCA for the 2024-2025 term and to consider any changes to the District's security coverage at a later time."*

The result? [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) abandoned the cuts, approved the full rate schedule, and kicked the can down the road *"to consider any revisions to the District's security coverage at a later time."*

#### 2. August 19, 2026 — Repeating the Delay at Grand Lakes MUD No. 4
More than two years later, at the [August 19, 2026 Grand Lakes MUD No. 4 Board Meeting](/assets/docs/20260819_GLMUD4-MM.pdf) (Minutes, p. 2), the exact same dynamic played out as the current Security Agreement approached its September 30, 2026 expiration.

While the boards were considering amending their multi-district *Law Enforcement Contribution Services Agreement*, [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) again stepped in to lock in another full year of unreduced spending for the 2026–2027 contract period (beginning October 1, 2026):

> *"Mr. Skinner informed the Board that MUD 1 and MUD 2 have agreed to renew the Security Agreement as presented and will consider making any changes in June of 2027, at which time each district will consider how to proceed for the 2027-2028 service term."*

Once again, the Board unanimously approved the 2026–2027 Security Rate Proposal on the spot—deferring any real scrutiny until June 2027. And notably, the [August 19, 2026](/assets/docs/20260819_GLMUD4-MM.pdf) minutes show the same meeting was used to tighten public records restrictions against residents scrutinizing these expenditures, including adopting a 36-hour annual cap on public information requests.

### The Strategic End-Game: Draining Cash Before 2034–2035
Why would a law firm work so hard to keep three water districts locked into $1.1M+ annual security contracts "for just one more year"? Look at the long-term financial clock ticking over Grand Lakes:

* **Cash Depletion Drives New Bond Debt:** Every million dollars drained from operating reserves on questionable recurring contracts reduces the cash available for capital maintenance—pushing the districts closer to issuing new debt. For water district attorneys, financial advisors, and engineers, bond issuances are major revenue events.
* **The 2034–2035 SPA and Annexation Horizon:** Grand Lakes’ Strategic Partnership Agreements (SPAs) with the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) expire in **2035**, requiring pivotal decisions on annexation by **2034**. If a MUD enters that window flush with abundant cash and no or low debt, the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) has every financial incentive to annex and dissolve the district—permanently terminating the private consultants' multi-decade contracts. Keeping district cash reserves depleted while carrying long-term bond obligations protects the status quo for the vendors, not the taxpayers.

### The Questions No Board Member Has the Courage to Ask
In every instance, [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) acts **not** as an impartial legal advisor protecting public funds, but as an active advocate ensuring these expensive, redundant contracts roll over uninterrupted. Yet not a single director across MUD 1, MUD 2, or MUD 4 has paused the meeting to ask the very obvious questions:

* **"What is your role here?"** A water district’s general counsel is hired to provide legal advice—not to lobby elected boards into rubber-stamping multi-hundred-thousand-dollar policing renewals.
* **"What do you or your firm benefit from hitting our water districts with these enormous, questionable expenses?"** Why is [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) so invested in preventing any reduction in deputy headcount or contract terms?
* **"Where is the basic economic sanity?"** As we documented in [Part I](./054_secsvcs-1), a single contract deputy under these arrangements costs Grand Lakes taxpayers more per year than the annual salary of many professors at Harvard, MIT, Princeton, Yale, Columbia, or Stanford.
* **"Would you ever pay 5 to 20 times market price out of your own pocket?"** No rational homeowner pays $1,000 a month for private home monitoring when comparable services cost $50 to $100 a month. Why, then, does [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) repeatedly urge public boards to renew bloated "additional" security contracts without competitive benchmarking, cost-benefit audits, or immediate reductions?

### Why "Next Year" Never Comes
Because [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) represents **all three** Grand Lakes MUDs simultaneously, [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) controls the flow of information between the boards. By telling MUD 2 in 2024 that MUD 1 already renewed, and telling MUD 4 in 2026 that MUD 1 and MUD 2 already renewed, [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) manufactures a closed loop where no single board feels able to act first.

📌 Until elected directors start questioning their attorney’s motives instead of deferring to his excuses, "considering changes next June" will remain a permanent mechanism for depleting Grand Lakes taxpayer funds. The harm to our 2,740-household community is enormous, long-lasting, and in the tens of millions of dollars. Accountability is well deserved. Justice is long overdue... ⚖️
