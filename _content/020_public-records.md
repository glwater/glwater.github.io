One of the most important principles governing local political subdivisions—water districts included—involves transparency.

Texas Government Code § [552.001](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.001) expresses the state's policy that the people are entitled to information concerning the affairs of government and the official acts of public officials and employees. The statute's fundamental principle: government is the servant, not the master, of the people.

That principle matters enormously for Grand Lakes. Residents cannot exercise meaningful oversight if they cannot obtain the documents necessary to understand how their government operates. Arbitrary, unwarranted defiance and denial by [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) attorneys ([Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) and [Gordon Chrsitopher Cranner](https://www.sphllp.com/_gordon_cranner)) made them withhold water district information for over a year. After many Public Information Act complaints, the [Office of the Texas Attorney General](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) issued on May 11, 2026, eight decisions\[<span style="color: #d72d47;">*</span>\] ordering [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) to release information he withheld since June 2025. A defiant attorney who disregarded the law for too long, the eight decisions were **not** deterrent enough. He continued with denial, arbitrariness, and indifference. That resulted in more complaints against said attorney to the [OAG](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/). On October 22, 2026, the OAG rendered two decisions (OR-26-007846-IC and OR-25-057172-IC) ordering [Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) to complete Open Records Training and provide a completion certificate within six months. While it is long overdue, the message indicates that the MUD attorney will face a strict disciplinary action should he choose to continue denial and defiance.

\[<span style="color: #d72d47;">*</span>\] OR-25-044591-IC, OR-25-051081-IC, OR-25-057685-IC, OR-26-000106-IC, OR-26-003417-IC, OR-26-004049-IC, OR-26-015987-IC, OR-26-023144-IC.

Whatever the ultimate legal conclusions in each individual matter are, a pattern of repeated disputes over access to basic governmental records is itself a reason for concern. A healthy public institution should not make transparency feel like a courtroom battle.

> 
Residents should be able to inspect the records necessary to understand:
- how much consultants are paid
- what contracts they have
- who authorized those contracts
- what work was performed
- what projects were approved
- how much projects cost
- what legal work was performed
- what governmental records exist
- what the district's financial condition actually is
- why rates, taxes, and proposed debt are increasing
- what litigation the district is involved in
- what legal fees have been paid over a certain period of time
- and more.
    
Transparency should be routine—not exceptional. The opposite means: the MUD attorney has much to fear; he has much to hide; and he recognizes his vulnerability. Concealing the district's records and hindering resident access to such records keep him safe!
