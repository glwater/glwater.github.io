When public oversight fails, residents pay the price—twice. First, taxpayers and homeowners fund the infrastructure through HOA assessments, MUD taxes, WCID taxes, and monthly utility bills. Second, when projects fail due to poor planning, botched execution, and absent supervision, residents end up footing the legal bills for both sides in court.

A lawsuit (**Cause No. 26-DCV-345440**) currently pending in the 502nd Judicial District Court of Fort Bend County—*Grand Lakes Community Association, Inc. v. Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District No. 2, Petra Construction, Costello LLC d/b/a 7Gen Planning, and Pape-Dawson*—lays bare the exact culture of finger-pointing, lax oversight, and consultant insulation documented across our community portal. Grand Lakes Community Association (the homeowners association \| HOA) is the Plaintiff, and the adverse parties are the Defendants.

### What Happened at the Hollow Trace Aquatics Facility?
According to Plaintiff’s First Amended Petition (filed July 9, 2026) and the sworn engineering affidavit of **Harry Beckwith III, P.E.** ([Waterscape Consultants, Inc.](http://www.waterscapeconsultants.com/)), the dispute centers on the rehabilitation of the Grand Lakes Phase 4 / Hollow Trace pool and 215-foot lazy river:

* **The Agreement (October 26, 2021):** The HOA entered into a Community Projects Agreement with **[Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/)** to repair and rehabilitate the lazy river, pool, and adjacent facilities. Under the contract, the MUD was responsible for supervising the project and ensuring the work was properly performed.
* **The Engineering & Design:** Plans were issued in February 2022 by **7Gen Planning** (a d/b/a of **Costello, LLC**, subsequently acquired by **[Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/)**). Additional wet-well drainage plans were issued by Costello in May 2022.
* **The Fatal Flaw—Removing Expansion Joints:** The original lazy river included exposed redwood expansion joints designed to absorb thermal expansion and soil movement. Although an August 2022 geotechnical report by Ninyo & Moore (acquired by [SOCOTEC](https://www.socotec.us/) in January 2025) noted the joints were leaking and *needed to be sealed*, the renovation directed by the District's engineer **completely removed the expansion joints** and poured a solid concrete connection.
* **The Resulting Damage:** Without expansion joints to dissipate thermal and soil stress, severe structural cracks tore across the pool floor, vertically up the walls, and into the perimeter beam.

### Independent Engineering Findings: "Extremely Poor Quality"
In his December 3, 2024 Professional Engineering Report (attached as Exhibit A to the July 9, 2026 Petition), licensed Texas Professional Engineer Harry Beckwith III documented major technical and regulatory failures:

* **Vague, Incomplete Plans:** The 7Gen Planning drawings omitted critical structural specifications—such as concrete thickness and rebar sizing/spacing—attempting to offload structural engineering responsibilities onto the pool contractor via a plan note.
* **Code Violations:** Detail 4 on Sheet L-1.2 proposed a skimmer with a submerged equalizer line—a design explicitly prohibited by the **Texas Administrative Code (TAC § 265.193(l)(1))** at the time the plans were produced.
* **Unsealed Engineering Drawings:** Costello's May 2022 drainage modification sheets were not signed and sealed by a licensed professional engineer.
* **Substandard Construction & Voids:** Core samples from a June 2023 geotechnical evaluation revealed the gunite pool floor was as thin as **4¼ inches** (below the 6-to-8-inch industry minimum), sitting atop a **2-inch void filled with trash and leaves**, with reinforcing steel exposed directly to soil and severely corroded.
* **Total Tear-Out Required:** Beckwith concluded that the only way to restore the pool to a safe, usable condition is to **completely remove the work installed under the 7Gen plans** and rebuild the floor, walls, and watertight expansion joints properly.

The HOA is seeking **$1,000,000 or more** in monetary relief, plus attorney's fees, expert costs, and interest.

### The Defense Playbook: Immunity, Technicalities, and Blame-Shifting
Rather than stepping up to make the community whole, every defendant responded with standard legal shields designed to evade accountability:

* **[Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/)** filed a General Denial and invoked **governmental immunity**, arguing the court lacks jurisdiction to hold a political subdivision liable for breaching its supervision duties—while simultaneously asking the court to award the District its legal costs against the HOA.
* **[Pape-Dawson](https://www.pape-dawson.com/) and Costello / 7Gen Planning** denied all liability, argued the statute of limitations had expired, blamed contractor installation errors, and asserted that the HOA or third parties failed to exercise the *"degree of care and caution that would have been used by a person with ordinary prudence"*.
* **[Petra Engineering LLC](https://petrallc.com/)** (sued as Petra Construction) filed verified denials disputing the legal entity name, argued the HOA lacked contractual standing to sue it directly, and shifted blame onto the MUD, Costello, 7Gen, Pape-Dawson, and the HOA.

### The Bottom Line for Grand Lakes Residents
This case raises fundamental questions that every taxpayer in Grand Lakes should be asking:

* **What are engineering consultants paid for?** If a firm collects hundreds of thousands of dollars in public fees yet disclaims responsibility for vague plans, unsealed drawings, ignored geotechnical recommendations, and absent field inspection—what value are taxpayers receiving?
* **Where was project governance?** Basic capital project management requires pre-qualification, clear technical specifications, active site inspections, punch-list enforcement, and liquidated damages—not rubber-stamped change orders and post-failure finger-pointing.
* **Who pays for the litigation?** Whether the HOA wins, loses, or settles, **Grand Lakes residents fund both sides: the HOA and the MUD**. Our HOA dues pay the Plaintiff's attorneys ([Roberts Markel Weinberg Butler Hailey](https://rmwbh.com/)), and our MUD taxes and water rates pay the MUD's counsel [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) that will exploit the opportunity to the fullest and claim endless coordination time with the defense counsel [Harris Hilburn](https://www.hhstxlaw.com/), installed by the insurance company.

📌 Accountability cannot remain optional when tens of millions of public dollars are at stake. Residents deserve transparent contracting, rigorous engineering oversight, and directors who put public duty ahead of entrenched consultants.
