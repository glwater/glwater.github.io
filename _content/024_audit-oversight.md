A good number of important points are buried within the [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.us/) [FY2025 audit report](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/static/aca54eeea16d936f13749fa12691dea7/Audit_FYE_2025_Final_Audit_Report_GLMUD_2_1_a83e444f68.pdf) for Grand Lakes MUD No. 2.

The auditor states that the district's management is responsible for the design and implementation of internal controls. Consultants may participate in the control system, but consultants are not the district's management. This distinction goes to the heart of the Grand Lakes water district problem. 

A public board cannot say: “We hired professionals, so we are no longer responsible.” Professional expertise does not eliminate public responsibility. Indeed, the more complicated the district's finances become, the more important independent oversight becomes.

> 
A board should be asking many, serious questions. The questions are not hostile to consultants. They are the basic questions of responsible government, especially given the remarkable violations we highlighted in the article on [Financial Controls](./019_financial-controls).
- What did we receive?
- Why did it cost this much?
- Was it necessary?
- Was the price competitively tested?
- Did another district obtain the same service for less?
- Does the consultant have a financial or professional relationship that could affect independence?
- Was the expenditure authorized in a properly noticed public meeting?
- Can the public inspect the supporting documents?
