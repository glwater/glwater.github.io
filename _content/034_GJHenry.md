Municipal Utility Districts (MUDs) are granted unique, sweeping governance powers under Texas law, including the authority to manage public utilities, levy property taxes, and issue millions of dollars in public debt. However, because these entities operate with lower public visibility than standard city councils, they remain uniquely vulnerable to systemic conflicts of interest and insulation from public scrutiny. 

The historical timeline of **[Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) (MUD 2)** in Katy, Fort Bend County, serves as a case study in how public funds, private property management firms, and district legal counsel can intertwine to the detriment of local taxpayers.

### The Core Conflict: Texas Water Code § 49.052
In 2023, an arrangement was finalized that blurred the legal boundaries of ethical municipal governance. While **Gregory Jay Henry** was actively serving as the Secretary of the [Grand Lakes MUD 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) Board of Directors, his private partnership, **[KPM Management](https://kpmcommunities.com/)**, was awarded a contract by the Grand Lakes Community Association (GLCA) as the Managing Agent for the entire community.

<img src="/assets/docs/coi.png">

This arrangement placed a sitting public board officer in a position of direct financial control over a **$4.8 million annual budget** generated by the community's residents, including:
* **$3.822 million** in annual HOA fees.
* Over **$500,000** in joint MUD-HOA shared-project funds.
* **$450,000** transferred from the MUDs to the HOA under a highly controversial "Additional Sheriff Security Services" arrangement. This amount is on top of an even higher amount paid by the three Grand Lakes MUDs directly to Fort Bend County in exchange for another highly controversial "Additional Constable Security Services" arrangement. MUD attorney, [Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) of [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/), devised said arrangements to deplete the MUD2 reserves and force the MUD into bonds, thus putting the consultants in control of the [district](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) for 40 more years. Residents / taxpayers managed to defeat the bond authorization in the <a href="https://results.enr.clarityelections.com/TX/Fort_Bend/126197/web.345435/#/summary?category=C_3">May 2, 2026, election.</a> 

### Statutory Disqualification
Under **Texas Water Code § [49.052](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.49&artSec=49.052) ("Disqualification of Directors")**, a board member is legally disqualified from serving if they experience a conflict of interest of this magnitude with regional management contracts. By explicit letter of the law, the remaining members of the MUD 2 board should have voted to remove Henry from his seat. They did not, choosing instead to maintain institutional silence after having worked in the background together with the MUD attorney, [Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) of [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/), to enable the conflicted director (Gregory Jay Henry) to arrange a Special Board Meeting on September 18, 2023, for his private company, [KPM Management](https://kpmcommunities.com/), to discuss infrastructure projects with the Home Owner Association and the MUD at the same time.

This is nothing short of appalling because the MUD attorney, MUD board, and the conflicted director (Gregory Jay Henry) knew what they were doing, but did it anyway, not bothered that someone, someday will put it all together and say: "Wow; how courageous those people are! What did each of them get in the background from the $4.8M deal?"

### Anatomy of a Shared-Project Vote: September 18, 2023
The practical complications of this conflict were made visible during consecutive board meetings late in 2023:

1. **The Set Up (August 21, 2023):** The MUD board authorized its own Secretary (Henry) to schedule a special joint session with the GLCA board to negotiate infrastructure funding allocations.
2. **The Dual Negotiation (September 18, 2023):** During the Special Public Meeting, Henry acted in a dual capacity—officially recorded as the MUD Board Secretary while simultaneously operating as a [KPM Management](https://kpmcommunities.com/) representative on behalf of the HOA. 
3. **The Demand for Funds:** At this meeting, [KPM Management](https://kpmcommunities.com/) presented a project cost summary totaling $505,918. Kyle Henry (representing [KPM Management](https://kpmcommunities.com/)) requested a formal commitment letter from the MUD to lock public district funds into the private homeowner association's accounts.
4. **The Allocation:** The MUD board subsequently approved a public reimbursement of up to **$250,000** to the HOA.

### The Illusion of Abstention
While Henry abstained from the final financial vote, structural analysis indicates that simple recusal does not cure deep-seated conflicts. Did Henry arrange everything around the dinner table, the night before with his-presumably-son Kyle? What kind of ridicule would the recusal then represent?

A conflicted officer participating in the structural design of budgets, backroom project choices, and the scheduling of special funding sessions still exerts significant operational pressure on the board's voting dynamic. 

### Conclusion and Policy Recommendations
The financial vulnerabilities exposed at Grand Lakes MUD 2 highlight the urgent need for broader statutory reforms regarding how utility districts operate throughout Texas:

* **Strict Ban on Concurrent Service:** Amending the Water Code to ensure that no sitting MUD board official can own, operate, or be employed by a management agency contracted by a concurrent community association within the same geographic boundaries.
* **Independent Budget Auditing:** Requiring third-party independent state comptroller audits for any joint project allocations exceeding $100,000 between a MUD and an HOA.
* **Proactive Digital Recordkeeping:** Forcing all utility districts to publish comprehensive meeting minutes, contract allocations, and engineer spreadsheets to an open, searchable digital repository within 7 business days of completion.

Taxpayers must remain actively engaged in reviewing their local special district operations. When public watchdogs fall silent, it falls on the community to demand systemic accountability.
