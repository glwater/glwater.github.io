<blockquote markdown="1">
The Elected Directors of the Grand Lakes Water Districts:

You stepped forward to run for office because you care about our neighborhood, our infrastructure, and our shared future. You are our neighbors. We see you in the grocery store, we walk the same trails, and we pay the same water bills. When you took your oath of office on Day 1, you accepted a profound title: **Public Servant**.

Yet, a deeply troubling structure has taken root across the Grand Lakes water districts. Upon taking office, directors are routinely presented with a form—under Texas Government Code § [552.024](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=552.024) or related provisions—allowing public officials to withhold their home addresses and personal phone numbers from public records requests. While designed to protect public safety, this provision is being fundamentally abused by entrenched private law firms.

By convincing all twenty Grand Lakes board members to sign these nondisclosure forms, legal counsel effectively cuts off the community. When a resident visits our neighborhood MUD website, they do not find a way to contact their elected neighbors. Instead, they are directed entirely to a corporate phone number and a physical office building some 30 miles away in downtown Houston.

### <span style="color: #1e3a8a;">The Consultant Shield: Who Does Silence Protect?</span>
When you make yourself entirely inaccessible to the public, you are not protecting yourself—you are protecting your consultants.

[Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) has weaponized this exact isolation tactic for decades. By positioning themselves as an authoritarian gatekeeper, they ensure that every piece of resident feedback, every budget complaint, and every question about escalating fees is filtered through them first.

This creates a dangerous environment where: 
- **The Board is Isolated:** You only hear what the consultants want you to hear, rubber-stamping proposals without a true pulse on grassroots community frustration.

- **The Taxpayer is Silenced:** Residents are left entirely vulnerable to exploitation by private-firm consultants who face no direct accountability, knowing it can take decades for structural overcharging to ever face justice.

- **Trust is Destroyed:** It creates a bizarre paradox where community leaders want the honor of public service, but choose to remain in hiding from the very constituents they represent.

### <span style="color: #1e3a8a;">A Model of True Leadership: The Nottingham Country MUD Example</span>
True transparency is not impossible; it is simply rare. Right next door, [Nottingham Country MUD](https://www.nottinghammud.com/) stands as a shining example of what authentic local governance looks like.

On their public directory, their five board members operate with true honor. They do not hide behind a law firm's corporate email filter. They explicitly provide direct channels for the public to reach them. They trust their community, and because they are accessible, their community can trust them.

### <span style="color: #1e3a8a;">Our Call to Action: Tear Down the Filter</span>
We urge all twenty Grand Lakes board members to rethink this strange, inherited practice. Break free from the corporate script handed to you by your consultants on day one.

You do not need to publish your private home address to be accessible. We challenge you, however, to create district-related email addresses (e.g., director.glmudx@isp.com) that only you access, *not* the law firm that wants you isolated.

We ask you to encourage the public to email you directly with the issues dear to their hearts. Invite scrutiny, welcome conversation, and look your neighbors in the eye. Let's return the "public" to public service. Thank you <img src="/assets/imgs/!p.png">
</blockquote>
