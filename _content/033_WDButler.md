[Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) current Board comprises the following five members: John Scot Vann, William Donald Butler, Gregory Jay Henry, George Armistead, and Elizabeth Drinkard. Armistead and Drinkard replaced Michael Christopher McClusky and Bradley Allen Baker, who lost in the <a href="https://results.enr.clarityelections.com/TX/Fort_Bend/126197/web.345435/#/summary?category=C_3">May 2, 2026, election.</a>

Municipal Utility Districts (MUDs) hold a unique responsibility. They manage the essential water, drainage, and infrastructure of our neighborhoods utilizing public funds collected straight from property taxes. For independent governance to function, these boards must maintain absolute separation from the vendors, contractors, and banks they hire to handle public capital. 

At [Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) in Katy, Texas, that separation has entirely eroded. A deeply entrenched conflict of interest involving a prominent board executive and [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/) of Houston raises critical questions about whose interests are truly being protected: local taxpayers or corporate bank balances.

### From Next-Door Neighbors to Board Appointments
The governance issues at [Grand Lakes MUD 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) trace back to an insular circle of local leadership. Longtime board president Michael Christopher McClusky managed the district since 2002. Over a twenty-four-year tenure, critics argue McClusky treated the MUD as a vehicle for personal employment while facilitating outside developer schemes to the financial detriment of Katy taxpayers. 

Though McClusky was finally unseated by residents in the <a href="https://results.enr.clarityelections.com/TX/Fort_Bend/126197/web.345435/#/summary?category=C_3">election on May 2, 2026</a>, his legacy remains active through his neighbor directly next door: **William Donald Butler**.

Butler did not earn his seat on the MUD 2 board through a public ballot. He was brought onto the board through an internal appointment after former member Tammy Fruge exited. Today, Butler serves as the Vice President of the Grand Lakes MUD 2 board—while simultaneously maintaining a corporate career as the Vice President of Community/Commercial Banking at [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/).

### The $240,000 Cushion: Who Benefits?
The direct intersection of Butler’s public board position and private corporate employment creates immediate concerns regarding how [Grand Lakes MUD 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) manages its money. Under a motion originally introduced by Director Bradley Allen Baker in July 2021, [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/) serves as the MUD’s official financial depository. Bur, [Bert Baker](https://www.cbhou.com/About/leadership) serves as Central Bank's "Senior Executive Vice President Chief Commercial Banking Officer." Is this a coincidence? Are the two related? If there is a conflict, was it ever disclosed and where? 

For years, managed by the MUD’s bookkeeper, Taylor Watson of [Municipal Accounts & Consulting](https://municipalaccounts.com/), the district's daily operational balance at Central Bank has been maintained at an unjustifiably high level of **$240,000**. But financial accounting standards and local cash-flow requirements entail that proper scheduling of checks and deposits could easily minimize this daily cushion to just **$25,000**. The remaining capital could be directed into optimized, interest-bearing accounts or overnight automated sweeps. Despite having the precise professional banking profile required to identify and fix this inefficiency, [MUD](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) Board VP William Donald Butler remains entirely silent. He has never advised the [MUD](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) on cash flow optimization, automated overnight sweeps, or reducing the bloated cushion held at [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/). 

To an independent observer, the reason for this silence is obvious: Butler is a corporate employee of the depository. Keeping large, idle public balances sits squarely in the financial interest of [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/)—the institution that funds Butler’s monthly salary and determines his annual corporate bonus. A signed [Conflicts Disclosure Statement](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/static/5d66ad0e59d1c136e109be55b36df060/Form_CIS_William_Butler_Feb_18_2025_391eef8334.pdf) is purely fictitious—it cannot erase the reality that an official cannot serve two masters. It does not answer the very basic questions that a rational, independent observe would pose.
- As a [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/) employee and board member of a [MUD](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) that banks with [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/), does Butler do anything (transfer information, share intelligence, etc.) that benefits his employer? Does he take/make any decisions that benefit his employer to the MUD's detriment?
- What kind of clients does Butler deal with as part of his employment with [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/)?
- Is Butler on any bank committee that deals with municipal or other forms of governmental clients?
- Does Butler have access to the MUD's account at [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/)?
- What [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/) metrics decide Butler's annual bonus?

### Corporate Deflection and Regulatory Escalation
When formally confronted with queries regarding compliance, potential familial ties between the [MUD](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) board and [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/) executives, and Butler's access to district transactions, Central Bank leadership chose corporate shield tactics over public transparency. 

In a statement, **Genevieve Ischy**, Executive Vice President and Director of Internal Audit & Compliance at [Central Bank](https://www.cbhou.com/), deflected accountability entirely, stating: *"Any questions regarding Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District No. 2, its board members, depository relationships, account management, or related governance matters should be directed to the District or its legal counsel."*

By claiming that an active Vice President's position on a client's public tax board is strictly a "MUD issue," Central Bank's compliance department deliberately ignores the systemic ethical vulnerability of its own operations.

Because local compliance avenues have been stone-walled by bank leadership, the issue is escalating. The **[Texas Department of Banking](https://dob.texas.gov/)** is obliged to investigate Central Bank’s compliance oversight, its municipal account handling, and the internal ethical guardrails governing its executive employees.

Taxpayer money should not serve as a quiet benefit package for insider banking relationships. Grand Lakes residents deserve clean, unconflicted financial stewardship.
