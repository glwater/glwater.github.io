Texas open-government requirements govern the manner in which governmental bodies deliberate and act. The attitude of [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) attorneys ([Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) and [Gordon Chrsitopher Cranner](https://www.sphllp.com/_gordon_cranner)) make the Grand Lakes residents raise questions concerning the preparation of board meeting agendas and minutes. The manner in which said attorneys administer the Grand Lakes water districts requires independent review for compliance with the open-government principles. This need is driven by unlawful practices that hinder resident access to information in order for said attorneys to keep tight control of the four Grand Lakes water districts.

- By going over the district's monthly meeting minutes, we regularly read the following, unmistakably meaning that no new items were decided added to the agenda of the next monthly meeting. But then, by inspecting the agenda for the following monthly meeting, we surprisingly find items like: "Consider amending the rate order." The MUD attorney would have unlawfully and unilaterally added the item to the monthly meeting agenda as he is constantly dreaming of making available extra funds that the MUD consultants can tap into through unwarranted administrative fees. This overreach must not be allowed.
>
<u>FUTURE AGENDA ITEMS</u>: The Board considered matters to be placed on future Board meeting agendas and noted that there were no additional items other than those set forth hereinabove."

These are not merely technical questions. The agenda is the mechanism through which the public learns what its government intends to discuss and decide. If an item can be introduced without proper notice, the public's opportunity to participate is diminished. Proper notice does not mean that the MUD attorney is at will to include or exclude items in/from the agenda. Matters appearing in subsequent agendas even though previous monthly meeting minutes indicate said matters had not been authorized by the board, mean that it is the MUD attorney who unlawfully included such agenda items without lawful authorization perfected in the previous meeting. This overreach is unlawful and must not be allowed, because the motives behind the MUD attorney introducing the agenda items are not known.

- Similarly, if minutes do not accurately document what occurred, the historical record of government becomes unreliable. And there are tens of examples that fall within this category. For example, time and again, the minutes would read: "Person X posed a question (or addressed the board). Person Y responded." This futility and ridicule are not coincidental. They are consciously intended to conceal information.

- And if exhibits associated with minutes are routinely unavailable, residents become unable to understand the decisions reflected in those minutes. Here again, this unlawful practice has been ongoing for many years—the [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) attorneys reference a number of exhibits in the board meeting minutes, but they never attach the exhibits to the published minutes. Residents who insist that the exhibits are public and must be made available become forced to file a Public Information Request (PIR). The PIR ends up as a complaint filed with the Office of the Attorney General, with a resolution taking as long as 12-18 months. During this period, the [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) attorneys unlawfully charge tens of hours. 

The community therefore has a straightforward demand:  
**Board agendas, minutes, exhibits, resolutions, orders, contracts, and supporting documentation should be complete, accurate, timely, accessible, and independently verifiable.**
