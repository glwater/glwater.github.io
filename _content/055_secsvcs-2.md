In [Part I](./054_secsvcs-1), we documented how Grand Lakes MUDs spent **$3.16 million** between FY2023 and FY2025 on supplemental Sheriff and Constable contracts—with MUD No. 2 paying **$1.79 million** of that total. Public records reveal how those contracts were promoted behind the scenes and how county officials later sought to shield records of the gathering from public view.

### The May 1, 2025 Dinner at Carrabba's
According to page 6 of the [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2 April 21, 2025 board meeting minutes](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/static/b822e8ac1d391d5c5a836b8146e348cd/Minutes_April_4_2025_amended_18bd95184e.pdf) (published May 27, 2025), Board President Michael Christopher McClusky reminded directors of an upcoming dinner hosted by [Fort Bend County Precinct 1 Commissioner Vincent Morales](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/commissioners-court/commissioner-precinct-1) for all Precinct 1 Special Purpose District board members on *hursday, May 1, 2025, at 6:00 p.m. at Carrabba's Italian Grill in Katy.

During the event, law enforcement representatives from Fort Bend County addressed water district directors regarding local theft and supplemental security patrols—an issue directly tied to the **$1.1 million annual security contracts** funded by Grand Lakes taxpayers.

### Withholding Records Under a "Campaign Event" Claim
When residents submitted a [Texas Public Information Act](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=) request seeking invitations, attendee lists, and communications surrounding the May 1, 2025 dinner, the [Fort Bend County Attorney's Office](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/county-attorney) asked the [Office of the Attorney General (OAG)](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/) to withhold the records, asserting that:

- The May 1, 2025 dinner was a **campaign event** rather than official county business.
- The county employee who sent the invitations acted solely as a "campaign event coordinator" outside her official capacity.
- Speakers at the dinner had no official county connection to the business of the water districts.
- No county funds were expended on the event.

### Contradictions in the Public Timeline
The county's assertions raise serious factual and legal questions under the [Texas Public Information Act](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=):

* **No Active Campaign:** [Commissioner Morales](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/commissioners-court/commissioner-precinct-1) won re-election unopposed in **November 2024** and began his third term on **January 1, 2025**. In May 2025, he was four months into a four-year term with no active campaign.
* **Official County Roles:** County staff members who coordinated logistics and name badges are salaried employees of [Fort Bend County Precinct 1](https://www.fortbendcountytx.gov/government/departments/commissioners-court/commissioner-precinct-1/precinct-1-staff), not campaign staff. Under [Texas Government Code Chapter 552](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.552&artSec=), records created or maintained by public employees in connection with official county business are subject to disclosure regardless of whether a private email address was used.
* **Direct Connection to Public Contracts:** Hosting elected water district directors alongside county law enforcement personnel to discuss county-provided security (Sheriff and Constable) patrols directly involves the transaction of official county and district business.

📌 Residents deserve full transparency regarding how **$1.1 million a year** in supplemental policing contracts are pitched, negotiated, and renewed—and why county attorneys worked to keep records of a dinner with water district officials out of public view.
