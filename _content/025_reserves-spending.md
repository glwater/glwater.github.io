Financial data for the Grand Lakes water districts raises serious concerns about the relationship between spending, reserves, and proposed borrowing.

Grand Lakes MUD No. 2 ended FY2025 with $5.96 million in reserves against $3.98 million in annual expenditures. Grand Lakes MUD No. 2's reserves have been declining ($9.135M in FY2023, $7.822M in FY2024, $5.963M in FY2025) while expenditures have remained unusually high. That matters because depletion of reserves creates a predictable political argument. And the situation becomes much more serious when/if the depletion is consciously meant to create that argument.  
> 
**The district needs money, therefore rates must rise—or taxes must rise—or bonds must be issued.**

![](/assets/docs/res.png)

Sure enough, with greed and an absent and indifferent board, the MUD attorney, [Christopher Todd Skinner](https://www.sphllp.com/_chris_skinner) of [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/), pursued all three avenues at once.  
- **Tax-rate hike:** On top of the increased property valuation, Grand Lakes MUD2 has already increased the tax rate by the 3.5% maximum allowable increase without an election, from 0.128% (2025) to 0.130466% (2026). Passed and adopted on September 21, 2026, the Order on Levying Taxes is available as part of the [Evidentiary Documents](#evidentiary-documents).  
- **Water/wastewater rates:** A lot seems to have been ongoing in the background among the MUD attorney, MUD bookkeeper, and MUD operator to increase the water/wastewater rates. Given the statements officially available from the September 21 and October 19 board meetings, it is assumed that an amended rate order will be announced once the October 19, 2026, board meeting minutes are approved in the November 16, 2026 meeting.

### The Board's Unconditional Duty to Control Costs
A budget deficit does not automatically establish the necessity of new revenue or debt. The district's board has to demonstrate that it has controlled cost, terminated all unnecessary contracts, and left no stone unturned. Only then can residents make an informed decision about whether additional revenue is genuinely necessary.

> 
Before asking residents for more money, a responsible government should first demonstrate that it has:
- strengthened internal financial controls
- eliminated conflicts of interest
- eliminated unnecessary expenditures
- scrutinized each and every contract the district has
- scrutinized each and every project proposed by consultants
- demonstrated that proposed projects are necessary and reasonably priced
- investigated unusually high professional fees
- obtained competitive bids for each and every professional services contract.

Have the Grand Lakes water district boards done that?!
