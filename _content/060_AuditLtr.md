**To:** [Office of the City Controller](https://www.houstontx.gov/controller), [Office of the Mayor](https://www.houstontx.gov/mayor), [Planning and Development Department](https://www.houstontx.gov/planning), and [City Attorney](https://www.houstontx.gov/legal/about) — [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/)  
**Subject:** Formal Request for Immediate Audit Pursuant to SPA Section 5.06 — Grand Lakes MUD Nos. 1, 2, and 4 (Fort Bend County, TX)

Dear City Officials,  

Pursuant to Section 5.06 (City Audit Rights) of the 30-year Strategic Partnership Agreements (SPAs) between the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) and Grand Lakes Municipal Utility District Nos. 1, 2, and 4 (effective early 2005), residents of the Grand Lakes community respectfully request that the City immediately initiate a forensic compliance audit of all expenditures funded by Section 5.02 Sales and Use Tax revenues.

**1. Extraordinary $27.3M Windfall & Subsequent Reserve Depletion**  
Between FY2013 and FY2025, [Grand Lakes MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) and the [City of Houston](https://houstontx.gov/) each received $27,310,173 under the 50/50 split of the 1% Houston sales and use tax—approximately $22.8 million of which stemmed from accounting and reporting errors at [The Home Depot](https://www.homedepot.com/) (6850 S Fry Rd, Katy, TX 77494). Now that those reporting errors have ended and annual SPA receipts have fallen to ~$339,000, MUD No. 2’s reserves have been drawn down from $9,134,604 (FY2023) to $4,352,960 (October 2026)—an unsustainable burn rate of $1.3M to $1.9M per year.

**2. Indicators of Material Breach (SPA Sections 5.04, 8.01, and Article VI)**  
Despite being completely debt-free and only 64%–70% the size of MUDs 1 and 4, [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/) has recorded operating expenditures up to 46% higher than its neighboring districts, driven by:  
• Consultant, legal, and operator fee increases far exceeding CPI and inflation in a fully built-out community with zero new development.  
• Over $3.16M spent across FY2023–FY2025 on supplemental security contracts outside core water, wastewater, and drainage functions.  
• Financial trajectories designed to exhaust reserves ahead of the March 2035 full-purpose annexation conversion window and justify new long-term bond debt.

**3. Requested City Action**  
Because MUDs 1, 2, and 4 share common legal counsel ([Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/)), operators, and multi-district cost-sharing agreements, we urge the City to:  
- Exercise Section 5.06 across all three districts ([MUD No. 1](https://www.grandlakesmud1.com/), [MUD No. 2](https://www.grandlakesmud2.com/), and [MUD No. 4](https://www.grandlakesmud4.com/)) to audit Section 5.02 sales tax expenditures against Section 5.04 lawful-use mandates.
- Issue formal Section 8.01 compliance determinations and cure schedules where unauthorized expenditures or bad-faith depletion are found.
- Protect the City’s future infrastructure interest ahead of the March 2035 conversion date (or earlier annexation under Texas Local Government Code § [43.0751(h)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=LG&chapter=LG.43&artSec=43.0751)) by opposing any SPA extension or unnecessary bond issuance.

Supporting audit tables, SPA citations, and public records are documented on the Grand Lakes Community Portal (*see* the article "[The $27.3M Home Depot Windfall](./047_HD)"). We welcome the opportunity to provide the complete evidentiary file to the City Controller’s audit team.

Respectfully,  
Grand Lakes Community Portal
