In Part 1, we talked and the Mathematical Model and developed a Governance Matrix. The four quadrants of said matrix give us an especially nice way to explain other expressions, like $A \cup B$ and $A \triangle B$.

#### 2.1 $A \cup B$ ~ Compliance OR oversight
At least one of the two exists. This is a **minimum-condition zone** and not necessarily good governance. It includes:
> 
• compliance + oversight<br>
• compliance but no oversight<br>
• oversight but no compliance.

Only one quadrant is excluded: **no compliance or oversight**. So:

> **$A \cup B$ represents governance in which at least one of the two fundamental safeguards is present.**

#### 2.2 $A \triangle B$ ~ One but not the other ~ The Governance Gap
$A \triangle B$ is equivalent to $(A \setminus B) \cup (B \setminus A)$. This is the **mismatch**. It means:
> 
• **Exactly one of the two safeguards is present:**<br>
• compliance / governance **without** oversight / accountability, **or**<br>
• oversight / accountability **without** compliance / governance.

That makes the symmetric difference particularly useful visually. We could call it **The Governance Gap** because it identifies situations where compliance (lawful governance) and oversight (accountability) have become separated. And **the best case (both compliance and oversight are taking place) and the worst case (neither compliance nor oversight are taking place) both lie outside the "mismatch" region.**

#### 2.3 The Power of the "Complement"
The use of the bar (complement) notation is an excellent means of explaining the significance of the core pillars on Sound Governance. We present two examples next.

#### 2.4 $\overline {A \triangle B}$ ~ Both, or neither
$\overline {A \triangle B} = (A \cap B) \cup (\overline A \cap \overline B)$. So outside the governance gap/mismatch we just discussed are two very different situations:
>
• **The best case** ($A \cap B$) ~ Both are present: compliance / governance + oversight / accountability.<br>
• **The worst case** ($\overline A \cap \overline B$ or $\overline {A \cup B}$) ~ Neither is present: neither compliance / governance nor oversight / accountability.

#### 2.5 $\overline {A \cap B}$ ~ Anything lacking at least one of the two
**$\overline {A \cap B}$** means **NOT (compliance AND oversight)**. But, **$\overline {A \cap B} = \overline A \cup \overline B$**, which, in ordinary language, means: **If either compliance (lawful governance) is absent OR oversight (accountability) is absent, the combined standard has failed.** The logical proposition said it all, and in a manner much stronger than simply saying: "Something went wrong."
