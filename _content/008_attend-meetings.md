MUD boards legally must host open public meetings, usually held monthly. The Grand Lakes water districts have for the past few years been meeting at the offices of the law firm of [Schwartz, Page & Harding](https://www.sphllp.com/) in Houston.

Because meeting hours and locations can be highly inconvenient for working residents, appearing in numbers and asking the right questions is critical. This strategy guide is for you...

### Phase 1: Pre-Meeting Preparation
Residents planning to attend the district's monthly meeting benefit from some preparatory work. 
- Pull the Agenda: By law, the board must post the meeting agenda publicly at least three business days in advance ([Government Code § 551.043](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=GV&chapter=GV.551&artSec=551.043)). Look for specific line items you would be interested in exploring: "Operator’s Report," "Bookkeeper’s Report," "Engineer’s Report," "Attorney's Report," "Approval of Invoices," "Contract Renewal," "Tax Rates," "Access to Public Information," Monthly Meeting Minutes," etc.
- Coordinate Speakers: You only get a limited window during the Public Comment portion of the meeting (some minutes per resident). Do not overlap. Assign one person to ask about billing, one to ask about vendor bidding, one to ask about maintenance costs, one to ask about contracts, and so on.
    
### Phase 2: The Script (What to Ask During Public Comment)
Step up to the microphone, stay perfectly firm, and use targeted questions to put the board on notice:
- On Vendor Competition: "When was the last time the Master Service Agreement for our private utility operator was put out for a competitive public bid? Are we actively looking at other operators to ensure Grand Lakes residents are getting the lowest possible operational costs?"
- On Financial Compliance: "Regarding the monthly Operator’s Invoice, what independent auditing processes does this board use to verify that the repair and maintenance line items billed to us by the private firm are accurate and not marked up?"
- On Conflict of Interest: "Have all current board members and active engineering, legal, and operational vendors filed up-to-date Conflict of Interest Questionnaires (Form CIQ/CIS) with the district this year as mandated by Texas Local Government Code?"
- On Access to Public Information: "Where are the exhibits to the monthly minutes you publish on the district's website? How are you compliant with the Public Information Act and the people's right to know? Which provision in which law entitles you to withhold the exhibits and publish the minutes without such exhibits?"
- On Monthly Meeting Agendas: "Who drafts the monthly meeting agenda; why, how, and by what authority? How are monthly meeting matters added to the agenda; who adds them, by what authority? Where are the board decisions to add items to the monthly meeting agenda? By taking consecutive monthly meeting minutes and agenda, prove and document how the board decided adding the new items on the agenda in the prior month meeting (as shown accordingly in the minutes)."

### Phase 3: Post-Meeting Accountability
After taking part in a monthly meeting, follow up to show the board and consultants that you are serious about holding them accountable.
- Review the Minutes: Check the minutes for transparency, accuracy, and completeness. Insist that your questions and the corresponding answers get properly and lawfully reflected in the minutes. Reject empty statements like "Resident Z asked a question and board member X or consultant Y answered it." This means that the district has much to hide. Insist on the minutes recording every vote and discussion. 
- Exercise Your Right to Request a Local Venue: Under Texas Water Code § [49.062](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?tab=1&code=WA&chapter=WA.49&artSec=49.062), if five residents request it, the board becomes legally obliged to move their public meetings from a Houston corporate law office that is 30 miles away from our residences directly to an accessible location inside the Grand Lakes community boundaries.
